This chapter is a reference directory of communist, Marxist-Leninist and socialist parties. It records what each party says about itself in the documents it publishes, on its own website, or in its own newspaper or other publication, called its official organ below. A party’s own name is used throughout. If a party calls itself Marxist-Leninist, scientific socialist, communist or Trotskyist, that is the label reported here, even where an outsider would classify it differently. Where a party states no label, the entry gives the name alone. The purpose is to show how these organisations present themselves, not to decide which label is correct.

Three rules shape the entries. First, a party’s own published words take priority. Where a programme or constitution was available, it is the basis of the entry. Where it was not, the entry rests on an official website or organ, and the source column says so. Second, for parties with no official site, the directory uses their listing in the International Meeting of Communist and Workers’ Parties (IMCWP), a recurring forum of communist and workers’ parties whose meetings are indexed by its information service, Solidnet. A listing there establishes that a party exists and calls itself communist, but gives no founding date, membership figure or election result. Third, the status labels are descriptive. Ruling means the party leads the government, alone or as the dominant partner. Opposition means it contests elections and has held seats in parliament. Extra-parliamentary means it is organised but has no seats in parliament. Banned means the party says it operates under a legal ban. These labels describe, they do not approve.

Where no official source was available online, the entry leaves the founding date blank and names the basis in the source column. A small number of well-known parties have thinner entries for that reason. Dates appear only when an official source states them. Nothing here is a verdict on a party’s size, support or truthfulness.

The five ruling parties in detail#

Five parties rule states that officially describe themselves as socialist: China, Korea, Vietnam, Cuba and Laos. The accounts below use each party’s own constitutional and congress language, and describe it as the party’s claim.

Communist Party of China#

The party’s official name is 中国共产党 (Zhōngguó Gòngchǎndǎng), the Communist Party of China (CPC). Its report to the 20th National Congress, delivered on 16 October 2022, says that “the Communist Party of China has walked a hundred-year course of struggle” (中国共产党已走过百年奋斗历程), which puts the hundredth anniversary in 2022. The report was published in full by the State Council’s official portal (gov.cn, report to the 20th National Congress).

The party runs an official website, 共产党员网 / CPC Member Network, operated by its Central Organisation Department. The name and information at the top of the site, its masthead, presents the party’s rules and a database of its regulations, the 党内法规库. The Party Constitution, 2022 edition, has a General Programme and eleven chapters covering members, the organisational system, central and local bodies, grassroots bodies, cadres (trained party officials), discipline, the bodies that check party discipline, party groups, relations with the Communist Youth League, and the party emblem and flag. It also carries the 20th Congress resolution on the constitutional amendment and an audio version.

The 20th Congress report records that over the preceding decade the party “upheld Marxism-Leninism, Mao Zedong Thought, Deng Xiaoping Theory, the Theory of Three Represents and the Scientific Outlook on Development, and fully implemented the Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era” (坚持马克思列宁主义、毛泽东思想、邓小平理论、“三个代表“重要思想、科学发展观，全面贯彻新时代中国特色社会主义思想) (official report text). The Preamble of the state constitution, republished on the same official portal, gives the fuller sequence of these theories. The chapter on the Constitution of the People’s Republic of China reproduces that text word for word.

Xi Jinping delivered the 20th Congress report on behalf of the 19th Central Committee. The official member portal devoted to “习近平党建思想” (Xi Jinping Thought on Party Building) and “习近平新时代中国特色社会主义思想” (official portal) places him at the centre of current party doctrine. He serves as General Secretary of the party.

The party’s main statements of aims are all official. They include the Party Constitution, 2022 edition, the Report to the 20th National Congress, 16 October 2022, the Constitution of the People’s Republic of China in official English, whose Preamble defines the party’s guiding role, and the CPC member portal study materials on the 20th Congress. The CPC is the ruling party of the People’s Republic of China, and the state constitution affirms the “system of multiparty cooperation and political consultation under the leadership of the Communist Party of China”.

The CPC’s self-description sets the pattern for the other ruling parties. The party says it is “the vanguard of the Chinese working class, the vanguard of the Chinese people and the Chinese nation, and the leading core of the cause of socialism with Chinese characteristics” (party constitution, General Programme, as published on the official portal). Here vanguard means the leading section. The phrase joins a claim about class, a claim about the nation and a claim about political leadership, and it is reproduced in the state constitution’s Preamble. The 20th Congress report sets out a long-term aim, “continuing to strive for the great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation”, together with commitments to “the great unity of all the people” and to a “community with a shared future for humanity”. These are the party’s own formulations.

Workers’ Party of Korea#

The party’s official name is 조선로동당 (Chosŏn Rodongdang), the Workers’ Party of Korea (WPK). It marks its founding anniversary in early October. Its official organ, Rodong Sinmun, marked the anniversary with an item titled “Party Founding Museum Conveying Glorious History of Our Party’s Founding”, dated 8 October 2026, on its English-language front page.

The official organ supplies both the party’s label for its leader and his title. Its English front page headline reads “WPK General Secretary Kim Jong Un’s Revolutionary Activities”, and the front page places those activities at the head of the paper. The “Politics” section repeatedly uses the title “respected General Secretary Kim Jong Un” (Rodong Sinmun, official organ of the WPK Central Committee).

How the party describes itself in its official organ is revolutionary and socialist in vocabulary, and its leader is described there as General Secretary. The wording used in its constitution is found in its printed congress documents. Official material includes the Rodong Sinmun English front page, its Video section, and the Korean Central News Agency, the official state news agency. A related 1964 WPK statement on the Sino-Soviet dispute, “Thwart the Manoeuvres to Split the International Communist Movement”, is reproduced in the Marxists Internet Archive’s collection on the Sino-Soviet split (archive copy of the WPK statement). The WPK is the ruling party of the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea.

Communist Party of Vietnam#

The party’s official name is Đảng Cộng sản Việt Nam, the Communist Party of Vietnam (CPV). Its official portal and the English edition of its newspaper, Nhân Dân, are its official publications. Nhân Dân identifies itself as the voice of the party and publishes the full texts of party leaders’ reports and congress resolutions. The General Secretary is Tô Lâm. The official portal’s headline “Tổng Bí thư, Chủ tịch nước Tô Lâm hội đàm với Tổng thống Cộng hòa Azerbaijan Ilham Aliyev” (dangcongsan.vn) gives him the title Tổng Bí thư (General Secretary). The English edition renders the same office as “General Secretary of the Communist Party of Viet Nam (CPV) Central Committee and President To Lam” (Nhân Dân English).

The portal’s document section publishes the party’s platform and congress documents. The party describes itself as communist (Đảng Cộng sản), and its portal and newspaper carry its reports and resolutions. Current official statements stress socialist construction, national independence, and “a community with a shared future” in its foreign relations (official CPV portal, Nhân Dân English). Further official documents are listed at the CPV portal, in Nhân Dân English Politics, and in Nhân Dân English Video. The CPV is the ruling party of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam.

Communist Party of Cuba#

The party’s official name is Partido Comunista de Cuba (PCC). Its own history page gives a milestone sequence rather than a single foundation date. It lists “1961 Fundación del Partido Comunista de Cuba”, followed by “1965 Constitución del Comité Central del Partido Comunista de Cuba”, and the first party congress in 1975. That is what the party itself publishes. It differs from the date often given in secondary accounts, and the official sequence is used here.

The party’s website, www.pcc.cu, has sections for El PCC, Historia del PCC, Organismos Superiores de Dirección, Directorio del PCC, Documentos and Biblioteca. Its official organ is Granma, whose masthead calls it “Órgano oficial del Comité Central del Partido Comunista de Cuba”.

The First Secretary is Miguel Díaz-Canel Bermúdez, identified on the party’s own site as “el Primer Secretario del Comité Central del Partido Comunista de Cuba y Presidente de la República” (PCC front page). The site’s “Documentos Rectores” section (El PCC) publishes its governing documents. The party describes its mission as “el desarrollo de la economía nacional” together with defence of the revolution and socialism. It describes itself as a communist party and the leading party of the Cuban revolution.

Other official material includes the PCC history and milestone chronology, the PCC governing documents, the 9th PCC Congress portal, the PCC video library, and the PCC people directory. The PCC is the ruling party of the Republic of Cuba.

The PCC case shows why a directory follows each party’s own chronology. The official history page’s “1961 Fundación” entry is what this party publishes about itself. The “1965 Constitución del Comité Central” entry explains why other accounts emphasise 1965. Both are reported, and neither is made to agree with the other by an outside editor.

Lao People’s Revolutionary Party#

The party’s official name is ພັກປະຊາຊົນປະຕິວັດລາວ, the Lao People’s Revolutionary Party (LPRP). Its official organ is Pasaxon, ໜັງສືພິມ ປະຊາຊົນ, “The People’s Newspaper”, which carries official reports of party and state activity. The General Secretary is Thongloun Sisoulith. The official organ describes him as “ເລຂາທິການໃຫຍ່ ປະທານປະເທດແຫ່ງ ສປປ ລາວ”, General Secretary and President of the Lao PDR, in its report of his meeting with the Vietnamese General Secretary on 15 November 2026 (Pasaxon).

The party identifies itself as a people’s revolutionary party, and the official organ reports its activity in socialist and revolutionary terms. The LPRP is the ruling party of the Lao People’s Democratic Republic. No official party website was reachable; the official organ is the published source used here.

Regional directory#

Six tables list parties by region. In the Founded column, “not stated” means no founding date was given in an available official source. The Source column names what each entry rests on: a party programme or constitution, an official website or organ, an IMCWP listing alone, or no official source located. Where a description comes only from a party’s name, the table says so. The abbreviation column of earlier editions has been dropped, because several parties share the same initials and the column could not be used reliably. Abbreviations used below are spelled out at their first appearance.

International coordinating bodies#

IMCWP and Solidnet#

The International Meeting of Communist and Workers’ Parties is the main forum where these parties coordinate. Its information service, Solidnet, states its role as “News, documents and calls for action from communist and workers’ parties. The items are the responsibility of the authors” (Solidnet home). Solidnet maintains an indexed page for each meeting from the first through the twenty-fourth (IMCWP index).

The most recent meeting documented here is the 24th IMCWP, held 7 to 9 August 2026 in Havana, Cuba, with the theme “Defending and honouring the legacy of the Cuban Revolution on the centenary of Fidel’s birth; deepening solidarity with socialist Cuba; strengthening our joint actions; and uniting our forces for peace and against imperialist aggression.” The official page lists a Final Declaration and a Plan of Action, contributions from attending parties, and written contributions from parties that could not attend (24th IMCWP).

The host was the Communist Party of Cuba. The attending list spans every region covered in this chapter and includes the Communist Party of China, the Communist Party of Vietnam, the Lao People’s Revolutionary Party, the Communist Party of Greece, the Portuguese Communist Party, the French Communist Party, the German Communist Party, the Communist Party of Britain, the Communist Party USA, the Communist Party of India (Marxist), the Communist Party of India, the Japanese Communist Party, the Communist Party of Brazil, the Communist Party of Chile, the Communist Party of Argentina, the Workers’ Party of Ireland and the Union of Communists of Ukraine, among many others.

Two other categories on the same page must be kept separate from this attending list. The written contributions come from parties that did not attend, including the Communist Party of Bangladesh, the Communist Party of Sri Lanka, the Communist Party of Ireland and the Communist Party of Sweden. The category headed “Solidarity Statements” includes a statement from the Communist Party of the Russian Federation, “Не уроним Знамя борьбы!” The CPRF therefore appears in the solidarity statements, not among the delegations that attended. The Union of Communists of Ukraine, by contrast, is listed as an attendee.

The related Joint Statements page and Videos section are maintained on the same official service. A separate domain, imcwp.org, leads to a Russian-language list of documents of communist and workers’ parties from 1957, 1960 and later years (imcwp.org). It is not the Solidnet meeting portal, and the two should not be conflated.

World Socialist Movement#

The World Socialist Movement states: “The World Socialist Movement (or WSM) consists of working class people who have organised themselves democratically with one objective; to bring about a complete change in world society … Although small, we are made up of companion parties and groups in several countries” (WSM). Its British section, the SPGB, publishes the 1904 Object and Declaration of Principles quoted above. The WSM is a socialist international organisation, not a Leninist one. It is included because its own documents define the boundary between “socialist” and “Marxist-Leninist” self-descriptions.

Party self-descriptions and the labels they use#

The definitions below reproduce how parties use these terms themselves, not how outsiders classify them.

Marxism-Leninism. The CPC’s official report names Marxism-Leninism first in its sequence of guiding theories (20th Congress report). The KKE says it “is guided by the revolutionary worldview of Marxism-Leninism” (KKE Programme). The CPRF says it is “based on creative development of Marxism-Leninism” (CPRF About). CPI(M) says it “adopted the principles of Marxism-Leninism as the guide to action” (CPI(M) Programme). CPC(M-L) puts the term in its registered party name (CPC(M-L)). The common element is a claim to scientific working-class theory plus an organisational doctrine of the leading party.

Maoism and Marxism-Leninism-Maoism. CPI(Maoist) and CPN(Maoist Centre) use the term in their own names. The Shining Path question is discussed further below.

Hoxhaism. A label applied by outsiders to parties that uphold Enver Hoxha’s critique of “revisionism”, the idea that a party has abandoned revolutionary aims. No party in this directory uses the label for itself. The primary Hoxha texts are reproduced in the Marxists Internet Archive’s Hoxha archive. They do not make “Hoxhaism” a party’s own label.

Stalinism. No party in this directory uses the label for itself. The PCF’s own history page refers neutrally to “la révélation des crimes du stalinisme, en 1956” as a historical event in the party’s development (PCF Histoire). That is the party’s own wording, recorded as such, not as an ideological label.

Trotskyism. The Tunisian Workers’ Party tradition applies the label to itself. The SPGB’s rejection of “political leadership” is a different, non-Leninist socialist position.

Anti-revisionism. The KKE programme says the party “fought opportunism and liquidationism in its ranks” and “never renounced class struggle, the socialist revolution, the dictatorship of the proletariat” after 1989 to 1991 (KKE Programme). The KKE says it fought tendencies it considered a retreat from revolutionary aims, and that it kept its commitment to class struggle after 1989 to 1991. In these documents “revisionism” was a term of abuse between communist parties, not a described set of ideas. The 1963 to 1964 party statements reproduced in the Sino-Soviet collection (CPC proposal; WPK statement) show how the word was used in official party documents.

Scientific socialism. The JCP’s programme says the party was founded in 1922 “as a party with scientific socialism as its theoretical basis” (JCP Programme); the SKP of Finland says it relies on “the theory of scientific socialism created by Marx, Engels and Lenin” (SKP history). In these parties’ usage the term avoids the label “Marxism-Leninism” while claiming the same theoretical lineage.

Socialism with Chinese characteristics and Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era. The CPC’s own formulation, combining a developmental stage, the “primary stage of socialism”, with the party’s current guiding thought, as published in the 20th Congress report and the state constitution’s Preamble (20th Congress report).

National democratic revolution (NDR). The SACP’s programme describes the NDR as “the most effective path to socialism in the South” and records a shift in the party’s understanding of its relation to socialism (SACP programme PDF). This is the party’s own theoretical vocabulary, not an outside classification.

Common ownership and democratic control. The SPGB’s 1904 Object: “a system of society based upon the common ownership and democratic control of the means and instruments for producing and distributing wealth by and in the interest of the whole community” (SPGB declaration). The same document insists that “common ownership is not state ownership”, the sharpest statement of the difference between World Socialist Movement socialism and Marxist-Leninist state-centred socialism.

Notes on individual parties#

The tables give the outline. The notes below expand a few entries where a party’s own published material is detailed enough to say more.

Communist Party of Greece#

Founded in 1918, the KKE describes itself as “the conscious, organised, vanguard section of the working class” with the “strategic goal … the overthrow of capitalism and the building of socialism-communism” (Programme). It states that it “is guided by the revolutionary worldview of Marxism-Leninism” and that it has “never renounced class struggle, the socialist revolution, the dictatorship of the proletariat”. Its statute, passed at the 19th Congress in April 2013, repeats the class analysis and the strategic goal, and defines the party as “a revolutionary organisation of like-minded volunteers” (Statute). The website’s footer gives a physical address in Neo Ionia and the party’s phone number.

The English-language site at inter.kke.gr carries the party’s international statements. It is a separate hostname. The programme’s preface states: “Το ΚΚΕ ιδρύθηκε το 1918 … Είναι το συνειδητό, οργανωμένο, πρωτοπόρο τμήμα της εργατικής τάξης που έχει στρατηγικό στόχο την ανατροπή του καπιταλισμού και την οικοδόμηση του σοσιαλισμού - κομμουνισμού” (“The KKE was founded in 1918 … It is the conscious, organised, vanguard section of the working class whose strategic goal is the overthrow of capitalism and the building of socialism-communism”). The same text says “Το ΚΚΕ καθοδηγείται από την επαναστατική κοσμοθεωρία του μαρξισμού - λενινισμού” (“The KKE is guided by the revolutionary worldview of Marxism-Leninism”) (KKE Programme). Its statute, adopted at the 19th Congress on 11 to 14 April 2013, names as its strategic goal “the conquest of revolutionary workers’ power, the dictatorship of the proletariat, for socialist construction” (KKE Statute).

Portuguese Communist Party#

The PCP’s English site publishes its “Programme and Constitution approved by the 19th Congress” and an “About” page with a photo chronology and references to “Programme Constitution Democracy and Socialism, the value of [the] Abril [1974 Revolution]”. Its homepage menu includes “Secretary-General”, “Central Committee”, “Secretariat”, “Political Committee”, “Central Control Commission”, “Congresses”, and “International Meeting of Communist and Workers Parties” (PCP English). Its own YouTube channel is linked from the site (PCP channel). The PCP is one of the most active participants in the IMCWP and hosts a dedicated IMCWP section on its site.

French Communist Party#

The PCF’s official history page begins: “Décembre 1920 : le congrès de la SFIO, réuni à Tours, décide à une large majorité de rejoindre l’Internationale communiste, donnant naissance à la Section française de l’Internationale communiste (SFIC)” (Histoire du PCF). The same page records the party’s post-1946 strength, “plus de 800 000 adhérent·es en 1946”, its role in creating social security and nationalisations, its expulsion from government in 1947, and its subsequent debates. The site links the party’s official YouTube channel with the description “La chaîne officielle du Parti Communiste Français. Revue de média, congrès et contenus thématiques” (PCF).

German Communist Party#

The DKP’s own programme is organised into five sections: “I. Imperialismus heute”, “II. Der deutsche Imperialismus”, “III. Der Sozialismus, die historische Alternative zum Kapitalismus”, “IV. Unser Weg zum Sozialismus”, “V. Die Kräfte des Widerstands und des Fortschritts” (Parteiprogramm). Its “Über uns” page narrates the party’s lineage from the SPD of Marx and Engels through 1914 to the present (Über uns). The party publishes a statute (Statut) and the weekly UZ.

Spain: PCE and its splinters#

The PCE’s official site provides an in-house history at historia-del-pce. It includes its own account of the 1956 20th CPSU Congress, Carrillo’s “vía nacional al socialismo” formulation at the VI Congress in December 1959 and January 1960, and the party’s clandestine activity under Franco. The site links “El programa estratégico del PCE” and the “Archivo Histórico del PCE”. Two other organisations appear alongside the PCE in the official 24th IMCWP delegation list: “Partido Comunista de los Trabajadores de España” (PCTE) and “Partido Comunista de los Pueblos de España”, plus “Communists of Catalonia” (IMCWP record). The PCTE’s own site publishes its programme and links an official YouTube channel (PCTE channel). PCE(m-l) has no official online presence in this directory.

Communist Party of Britain and Socialist Party of Great Britain#

“What we stand for” states: “According to its rules, the Communist Party is guided by the theory and practice of Marxism-Leninism. This is far from being a fossilised set of ideas. Marxism-Leninism is a science…” The CPB also publishes “Britain’s Road to Socialism”, its “Party Rules and Standing Orders (PDF)”, and the resolutions of its 58th Congress in 2025 (CPB site). Its “Our History” page and “Congress” section are the party’s own. This is a Marxist-Leninist party that follows the “British road to socialism” programme.

The SPGB is not Marxist-Leninist, despite being a socialist party often grouped with communist parties by outsiders. Its 1904 “Object and Declaration of Principles” says the party’s object is “the establishment of a system of society based upon the common ownership and democratic control of the means and instruments for producing and distributing wealth by and in the interest of the whole community”, and adds: “Working class emancipation necessarily excludes the role of political leadership” and “Common ownership is not state ownership” (SPGB declaration). That is a refutation of the Leninist party model.

Communist Party USA and Party of Communists USA#

CPUSA publishes a constitution and a programme on its own site. Its constitution, dated 25 July 2025 on the party’s own site, states: “The roots of the Communist Party extend deep into our nation’s soil, even far beyond our founding in Chicago in 1919 … We apply the scientific outlook developed by Marx, Engels, Lenin and others in the context of U.S. history, culture and traditions” and commits the party to “Bill of Rights socialism” (CPUSA Constitution).

Its programme, The Road to Socialism USA, is described on the official site as “adopted in 2019 and updated for the 32nd National Convention in 2024 in Chicago and approved by the National Committee on July 13, 2025” (CPUSA Party Program). The text states: “We need to replace capitalism with socialism, a system of cooperation, democracy, and equality.” The site’s current framing emphasises working-class unity, opposition to white supremacy and male supremacy, and immigrant rights as conditions of that unity, in the party’s own words.

The separate Party of Communists USA has no official website in this directory. The two organisations are not merged merely because their English names are similar.

Communist Party of Canada and CPC(M-L)#

The Communist Party of Canada is listed as a participant at the 24th IMCWP (IMCWP record). CPC(M-L) maintains an active official site at cpcml.ca with an “About” page, a platform, and the statement that it is registered as the Marxist-Leninist Party of Canada. Its official site states plainly: “This website is operated by the Communist Party of Canada (Marxist-Leninist)” and “CPC(M-L) is registered by Elections Canada as Marxist-Leninist Party of Canada” (CPC(M-L)). Its “About” page links a “Platform of the Marxist-Leninist Party of Canada” and material on Hardial Bains (About). Both parties are recorded.

Communist Party of India (Marxist) and CPI(Maoist)#

CPI(M)’s programme is explicit: “The Party adopted the principles of Marxism-Leninism as the guide to action for winning national independence, to attain the objective of socialism and to advance towards the ultimate goal of communism”; it traces the communist movement’s “formation in 1920” and states that “The Communist Party of India (Marxist) was formed after a prolonged struggle against revisionism. It adopted the Party programme in 1964” (CPI(M) Programme). Here revisionism means the idea that a party has abandoned revolutionary aims. Its site publishes a “Party Constitution”, 24th Congress political and organisational reports, and a videos section (CPI(M) Videos). Its official YouTube channel is linked from the site (CPIM Speak).

CPI(Maoist) is listed with no official online source and no founding date, leader or programme. The 24th IMCWP list does not include CPI(Maoist).

Nepal#

The Nepali communist party with an official website is CPN(UML) at cpnuml.org. The site is in Nepali. Its front page identifies “नेकपा (एमाले)” (CPN(UML)), names K. P. Sharma Oli as chairperson (“अध्यक्ष”), lists the party’s central leadership and departments, and marks “नेकपाको गौरवशाली ७५ वर्ष” (“75 glorious years” of the party). Other Nepali Marxist-Leninist organisations, CPN(Maoist Centre), CPN(Unified Socialist) and smaller formations, have no official website here; they are noted but not given dates or leaders.

Communist Party of the Russian Federation and successor parties#

The CPRF’s “About Us” text states: “The CPRF, based on creative development of Marxism-Leninism, has as its main goal the building of socialism, a society of social justice espousing the principles of collectivism, freedom and equality … The Communist Party of the Russian Federation is the successor to the cause of the CPSU and the CP RSFSR, being their ideological heir” (CPRF About Us). The same page records the 13 February 1993 congress and March 1993 registration. Its official newspaper is Pravda, “founded on May 5, 1912”, as the page states.

On Soviet-era successor parties, the official IMCWP record places the “Communist Party of the Russian Federation” under Solidarity Statements, while the “Union of Communists of Ukraine” appears among the delegations that attended the 24th meeting (IMCWP record). The two categories are distinct.

Workers’ Party of Belgium, and the Nordic and Dutch parties#

The Workers’ Party of Belgium is listed at the 24th IMCWP (IMCWP record). Its official site was disabled at the time of writing.

The Dutch CPN describes itself on its official site as “Waar staat de CPN voor? Socialisme-communisme, en wat ons uniek maakt als partij” (CPN). It links an official YouTube channel (CPN channel). Sweden’s Kommunistiska Partiet publishes a detailed party history: the left opposition formed KFML in 1968, KFML(r) after the 1969 to 1970 miners’ strike, and “ombildades förbundet 1977 till partiet KPML(r)” (“the organisation was transformed in 1977 into the party KPML(r)”) (Partihistorik). Norway’s NKP maintains an official site with a history section (NKP; Historie). Finland’s SKP states that it “relies in its activity on the theory of scientific socialism created by Marx, Engels and Lenin” (“SKP nojaa toiminnassaan Marxin, Engelsin ja Leninin luomaan tieteellisen sosialismin teoriaan”) and documents its 1985 split (SKP history).

Austria, Switzerland and Italy#

Austria has two communist parties. The KPÖ’s official site (kpoe.at) presents housing, social affairs, peace and neutrality as its current priorities; its history section is at Geschichte der KPÖ. The Partei der Arbeit publishes a founding and principles declaration adopted unanimously at its founding congress on 12 October 2013 (Grundsatzerklärung).

For Switzerland, the 24th IMCWP list names a “Swiss Communist Party”. For Italy, the same list names both the “Italian Communist Party” and “Party of the Communist Refoundation (Italy)”. Both countries are recorded through the official IMCWP listing.

Turkey#

The TKP’s own site publishes “Temel Metinler” (basic texts), a “Program”, a “Toplumcu Anayasa” (social constitution), congress and conference texts, and a party management page, all reachable from its homepage (TKP). The site shows a YouTube playlist titled “Komünist Bakış”, “Komünist Bakış | Pazartesi, 21.30”, and individual videos on its own pages (TKP site).

Brazil: PCB and PCdoB#

PCdoB’s official history page marks a “Centenário” with a Central Committee session on 25 March 1922 in Niterói and the document “PCdoB: Um século de lutas em defesa do Brasil, da democracia e do socialismo”, covering “1922 to 2022” (PCdoB História). Its programme is at programa. The Brazilian Communist Party is at PCB.

Chile, Argentina, Uruguay and Peru#

The Chilean official history page states verbatim: “Partido político fundado el 4 de junio de 1912, en Iquique, con el nombre de Partido Obrero Socialista, el cual modificó en su IV Congreso, realizado el 2 de enero de 1922 en Rancagua, pasando a denominarse Partido Comunista de Chile” (PCCh Historia). It also records the periods of illegality, including the 1948 “ley maldita” and the Pinochet dictatorship’s persecution between 1973 and 1989, in the party’s own words.

The Peruvian website pcp.pe belongs to the Partido Comunista Peruano, whose site marks the “95 aniversario de la fundación” in an item dated 25 October 2023 (PCP). This is a distinct organisation from the Partido Comunista del Perú associated with Shining Path. The 24th IMCWP list includes both “Communist Party of Peru (Patria Roja)” and “Peruvian Communist Party” as separate delegations (official IMCWP record). The Uruguayan PCU’s official site marks “106 años del pueblo al futuro” and provides sections for its Central Committee, statute, congresses and publications (PCU). The anniversary counts are the parties’ own wording.

South Africa: SACP and EFF#

The SACP is documented in the Africa table. Its constitution, as amended by the 15th National Congress in July 2022 and published as a PDF on the party’s own site, states: “The name of the organisation is the South African Communist Party (SACP) and it is the successor in title to the Communist Party of South Africa which was founded in July 1921” (SACP Constitution, §1). The same document makes “Marxist-Leninist theory” an explicit obligation and speaks of “a Marxist-Leninist revolutionary morality and the four pillars of the Marxist-Leninist” outlook. The party’s site describes its alliance: “The SACP is a partner in the Tripartite Alliance consisting of the African National Congress and the Congress of South African Trade Union” (About SACP). Its programme, The South African Struggle for Socialism, is published on the same official domain (SACP programme PDF). Its constitution, programme and self-description all come from the party itself.

The Economic Freedom Fighters are not classified as Marxist-Leninist here.

Oceania#

The Oceania section is short. The one listed party with substantial material, the Communist Party of Australia, publishes its newspaper The Guardian on its own site and links an official YouTube channel (CPA). Its site’s own reporting of other parties’ anniversaries, such as “105th anniversary of the Communist Party of China” and congratulations to the Communist Party of Chile on its 114th, shows its international affiliations.

Historical and defunct parties#

Historic parties present themselves through successor organisations more often than through surviving websites. Their record usually survives either in a successor’s history pages, such as CPB, CPI(M) and DKP, or in an archive that reproduces their own publications.

  • Communist Party of the Soviet Union. Its own documents, the 1957 Declaration of Twelve Parties, the 1960 Statement of 81 Parties and the 1963 Open Letter, are reproduced in the Marxists Internet Archive collection used throughout this book: 1957 Declaration, 1960 Statement, CPSU Open Letter, 14 July 1963. These are official party texts in an archival reproduction, not a party website. The Comintern’s dissolution documents are in Degras, vol. 3 (PDF).
  • Communist Party of India before the split. The programme of CPI(M) contains the official account of the undivided party’s 1920 formation and its adoption of Marxism-Leninism (CPI(M) Programme). It is used here as an official successor party’s history of its own movement, not as an independent CPI document.
  • Communist Party of Great Britain and affiliated traditions. The Communist Party of Britain’s own history page presents the British communist tradition (CPB Our History). The CPGB’s historical newspaper archive is in the Marxists Internet Archive’s CPGB section, which reproduces party publications.
  • Partito Comunista Italiano, historical, 1921 to 1991. The Italian parties listed at the 24th IMCWP are current organisations.
  • Kommunistische Partei Deutschlands, historical. The DKP’s “Über uns” page gives the German communist movement’s lineage in the DKP’s own words (DKP); no official KPD site was located.
  • Workers’ Party of Ethiopia, Sudanese Communist Party’s historical congress documents, and similar formations. They are not given dates or leaders here.

The Shining Path question#

The organisation known as Shining Path is called Partido Comunista del Perú (PCP) in its own documents. It must be distinguished from the Partido Comunista Peruano (also PCP), whose official site is pcp.pe and which participated in the 24th IMCWP as the “Peruvian Communist Party” (IMCWP record). The two organisations are distinct, and the IMCWP lists them as separate delegations.

Comparing the five ruling parties#

The comparison is useful because “ruling communist party” is an umbrella term that can hide real differences. The official documents of the five parties show five distinct ways of stating the relation between party, state and society. None of these formulations is a translation of another; each is a national product.

China: leadership within a multiparty framework. The state constitution’s Preamble, published on the official State Council portal, says that “the system of multiparty cooperation and political consultation under the leadership of the Communist Party of China will continue and develop long into the future”. The same text calls the party “the vanguard of the Chinese working class, the vanguard of the Chinese people and the Chinese nation, and the leading core of the cause of socialism with Chinese characteristics”. The CPC’s own constitution, published by the party’s member portal, repeats the vanguard language and sets out eleven chapters governing members, organisation, central and local bodies, cadres, discipline, and the party emblem and flag (CPC Constitution). The distinctive feature is that party supremacy is stated together with a formal system of talks with other parties, phrased in a way the other four do not use.

Korea: the leader at the head of the official organ. The Rodong Sinmun English front page is headed “WPK General Secretary Kim Jong Un’s Revolutionary Activities”, and every subsequent section, News, Politics, Economy, Culture, History, Photo and Video, is framed by that heading (Rodong Sinmun). How the party describes itself is organised around the person of the General Secretary and his “revolutionary activities” rather than around a published constitutional paragraph. For the WPK, the official organ supplies leadership and programmatic tone.

Vietnam: party office and state office held together. The official CPV portal’s headline “Tổng Bí thư, Chủ tịch nước Tô Lâm”, General Secretary and President, joins the party office and the state office in a single title-line (CPV portal). Nhân Dân renders the same arrangement in English: “General Secretary of the Communist Party of Viet Nam (CPV) Central Committee and President To Lam” (Nhân Dân). The official record establishes both the party post and the state post.

Cuba: a chronology of institution-building. The PCC’s own history page does not offer a single founding date. It offers a sequence: 1961 “Fundación del Partido Comunista de Cuba”, 1963 and 1965 party construction in the armed forces and the interior ministry, 1965 “Constitución del Comité Central”, 1975 the First Congress, through to the Eighth Congress in 2021 (PCC history). The party’s site also names the First Secretary and President in the same sentence (PCC) and publishes its governing documents under “Documentos Rectores” (El PCC). Its self-description is incremental and institutional, centred on congresses and the party’s role in defending the revolution.

Laos: a party known through its newspaper. With no reachable party website, the LPRP is documented through Pasaxon, whose report of 15 November 2026 identifies Thongloun Sisoulith as “ເລຂາທິການໃຫຍ່ ປະທານປະເທດແຫ່ງ ສປປ ລາວ”, General Secretary and President of the Lao PDR (Pasaxon). Here the official organ is not merely a publicity outlet; it is the channel through which the party’s leadership and activity are known.

The record shows five different kinds of source. China publishes a party constitution alongside a state constitutional framework. Korea’s case rests on an official newspaper organised around the leader’s activity. Vietnam’s official portal joins party and state titles in a single headline. Cuba publishes a party website with a chronology and governing documents. Laos has no reachable party website and is known through its official newspaper alone. Treating all five alike would erase these differences.

The IMCWP record is also a reminder that the communist world is not a single bloc. The same meeting includes ruling parties such as the CPC, CPV, LPRP and PCC, parliamentary opposition parties such as KKE, PCP, PCF, CPRF and KSČM, extra-parliamentary parties such as DKP and CPB, and organisations that contest the label “reformist” or “revisionist” in their own documents. The coordinating body is real; agreement on doctrine is not.

Official video channels#

Most parties in this directory link a video channel from their own website. Those channels are the party’s own publishing choice, and a link from a party site shows where the party chose to speak. It does not show the party’s size or the truth of its claims. The full catalogue of movement and party channels belongs in the chapter on movement media, and the directory of recordings of particular figures belongs in the chapter on video and audiovisual sources. A few state examples illustrate the pattern here. The official organ of the WPK has a Video section carrying items such as “Picture of Loving Care” and “Under Paternal Love”. The CPC member portal posted a video item on the 20th Central Committee’s fifth plenary session, “中共中央新闻发布会介绍和解读党的二十届五中全会精神” (12371.cn video item). The English edition of Vietnam’s Nhân Dân runs a Video section with items on culture and national events. The PCC links a video library from its homepage. These are the parties’ own pages, cited in the detail sections above.
