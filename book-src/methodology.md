# Methodology and Research Disclosure

This page states how the book was made. It is written so that a reader can tell the difference between what has been checked, what has been asserted, and what remains open.

## What the book is

The book is a narrative history built around primary sources. Each chapter prints documents from movements, states, witnesses and critics, and sets them beside the scholarly arguments about what they mean. It does not offer a single verdict on socialism or capitalism. Its governing standard requires that the same evidentiary rules be applied to socialist governments, capitalist governments, movements, opponents and witnesses alike.

The book is not neutral in the sense of avoiding judgements. Where evidence establishes responsibility, it says so. Where evidence is thin, it says that too. Objectivity here means accurate evidence, consistent standards, transparent reasoning and conclusions proportionate to what the sources support.

## How it was researched

The manuscript was assembled with substantial AI assistance. Research agents located sources, extracted claims, checked URLs, compared translations and drafted text. Human editorial responsibility rests with the project author. There is no named team of historians, no university affiliation and no external academic peer review. Readers should not assume that a chapter has been read by a qualified specialist in its field.

A 20-reviewer internal pass and a separate audit process produced reports on every chapter. The audit extracted 7,189 material claims from 99 documents, of which 3,220 were classed as high-stakes. It produced 343 verification reports and 58 adversarial falsification reports. These are internal, AI-assisted checks. They are useful for finding inconsistencies, broken sources and unsupported claims. They do not establish historical authority.

## What has actually been verified

- URLs were checked by opening them and judging the content, not by trusting HTTP status. The URL audit covered 1,223 links; 1,110 were judged sound.
- Claims were checked against the cited pages where those pages could be opened. A match shows that a cited page contains the names, dates or numbers in a claim. It does not show that the claim is true, that the source is reliable, or that the interpretation is sound.
- 26 corrections were logged. Only six log entries cite an independent re-verification report, and one of those was marked for rework. The remainder rest on coordinator sign-off.
- 326 findings from the audit are recorded as still present in the manuscript. A subset of 17 high-confidence falsified, refuted or misattributed claims has now been corrected; the others await adjudication.
- A bibliography reconciliation found more than 1,300 chapter URLs that do not appear in the consolidated bibliography. This is a reconciliation task, not proof that those sources are unreliable.

No external academic reviewer has checked any chapter. No correction in the project is described as independently verified by a qualified historian.

## Editorial history

An early compiling document in the project's research files was written explicitly from within the Marxist-Leninist tradition and excluded anti-communist and intelligence material. That source rule was retired on 22 November 2026 and replaced by the current standard, which requires engagement with academic historiography and forbids suppressing credible evidence because it complicates a political interpretation.

Changing a policy does not rewrite the manuscript written under it. Twenty-five chapters carry a last-updated date of 22 November 2026 and should be read as the most likely to retain assumptions from the earlier approach. The other chapters were produced by the broadening programme after the decision. A systematic re-audit of the earlier chapters for inherited assumptions remains outstanding.

## Levels of certainty

The book distinguishes the following levels of confidence. These describe evidence, not political agreement.

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Established | Supported by strong, convergent evidence |
| Strongly supported | Substantial evidence with limited uncertainty |
| Probable | Best-supported interpretation, incomplete evidence |
| Contested | Credible scholarly disagreement exists |
| Interpretive | Analytical judgement rather than a directly verifiable fact |
| Uncertain | Available evidence is insufficient |
| Unverified | Verification has not been completed |
| Refuted | Available evidence contradicts the claim |

Political disagreement does not make a well-supported fact contested. A government denying an event is not equivalent to qualified historians disagreeing over its interpretation.

## Publication maturity

Chapters are at different stages of verification. The levels below describe that difference; no chapter is presented as certified.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| L0 | Research or reference draft |
| L1 | Initial manuscript; not fully verified |
| L2 | Source verification completed |
| L3 | Factual corrections applied and re-verified |
| L4 | Historiographical and framing review completed |
| L5 | Adversarial review completed |
| L6 | Independent specialist review completed |
| L7 | Certified against the project's published editorial standard |

At present no chapter is above L1. Certification at L7 would mean compliance with the project's own standard, not a guarantee of historical truth.

## Reporting a problem

A correction is only complete when it has been recorded, verified against the cited source, applied to the manuscript, published, and reconciled against its citations and dependent claims. Readers who find an error are invited to report it through the project repository.
