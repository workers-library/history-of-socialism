On 24 October 2017 the National Congress of the Communist Party of China (CPC) amended the Party Constitution to include Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era. The amendment placed a sitting leader's name alongside Marxism-Leninism, Mao Zedong Thought, Deng Xiaoping Theory, the Theory of Three Represents and the Scientific Outlook on Development in the party's formal guide for action. It also gave a name to what the party calls the new era, an official division of history into periods that now shapes party and state documents. This chapter explains how the official accounts present Xi's life and rise, what the thought and its associated slogans mean in those accounts, and how independent historians and reporters have interpreted the same period.

The documentary base is uneven. Official biographies, congress reports, constitutional texts and white papers survive in abundance, and they are quoted here as evidence of what the party and state said. They are not by themselves evidence that the policies worked. Where scholars have reached different conclusions, or where independent reporting and United Nations documents contradict official statements, that is stated in the text.

## Family and youth in the official biography

The basic facts come from a biographical notice issued by Xinhua at the time of Xi's third election as president. It states: "Xi Jinping, male, Han ethnicity, was born in June 1953 and is from Fuping, Shaanxi province. He began his first job in January 1969 and joined the Communist Party of China (CPC) in January 1974. Xi graduated from the School of Humanities and Social Sciences, Tsinghua University where he completed an in-service graduate program in Marxist theory and ideological and political education. He holds a Doctor of Law degree" ([Brief introduction of Xi Jinping, 10 March 2023](https://english.www.gov.cn/Homepage/202210/24/content_WS641af593c6d0f528699db898.html)). The same notice lists his four offices: General Secretary of the CPC Central Committee, Chairman of the CPC Central Military Commission, President of the People's Republic of China, and Chairman of the PRC Central Military Commission.

Official profiles describe the family as the place where his political formation began, a household in which Party service was treated as ordinary. They call his father, Xi Zhongxun, "a revered CPC leader", and quote Xi saying that his father had "devoted himself wholeheartedly to the Chinese people" and that he had pledged to follow in his footsteps ([Xi Jinping leads China on new journey, 25 October 2022](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/25/content_WS6357e5c6c6d0a757729e1c01.html)). A 2025 feature says both parents "participated in the revolution at a young age and were very dedicated to their work" and recalls that Xi Zhongxun took on childcare duties, including bathing the children and doing their laundry, when his wife Qi Xin worked far from home ([Xi Story: Women breaking free from household chores, 11 October 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/11/content_WS68e9be87c6d00ca5f9a06aec.html)). These are recollections published by state media decades after the events. They tell us how the official biography wants the family to be remembered.

Xi's first job began in January 1969, when he was not yet sixteen. The 2022 profile describes the scene: "At 15, as an 'educated youth,' Xi left Beijing for a village called Liangjiahe in an arid part of Northwest China's Shaanxi province, carrying with him a small sewing bag embroidered with Chinese characters 'mom's heart' by his mother Qi Xin" ([Xi Jinping leads China on new journey](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/25/content_WS6357e5c6c6d0a757729e1c01.html)). An educated youth was a city student sent to the countryside to work and learn from peasants during the Cultural Revolution, the political campaign that convulsed China from 1966 to 1976. The 2017 profile gives the dates: "He stayed in Liangjiahe, a small village in Northwest China's Shaanxi province, from 1969 to 1975" ([Profile: Xi Jinping and his era, 17 November 2017](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/top_news/2017/11/17/content_281475945550012.htm)).

The official account stresses physical work and hardship. It says he slept in cave dwellings, suffered flea bites, herded sheep, carried manure and hauled coal, and that he recalled wanting "to make it possible for the villagers to have meat and have it often". In his own words, quoted by Xinhua:

> "At that time, I did all kinds of work, reclaiming wasteland, farming, hoeing, herding, hauling coal, mounding, and carrying manure. I came to understand what reality, seeking truth and the masses meant. These gave me an enduring inspiration in my life."

Source: [Profile: Xi Jinping and his era, 17 November 2017](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/top_news/2017/11/17/content_281475945550012.htm).

A 2025 feature adds a dam building story in which he spent long hours compacting earth with heavy stone pounders. It presents his hands as blistered and bleeding and quotes unnamed locals calling him a fine lad ([Xi Story: From grit to greatness, 1 May 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202505/01/content_WS68137daec6d0868f4e8f23fc.html)). This is a retrospective official reconstruction, written decades after the event, with no named witness and no indication of when the recollection was recorded. It is not contemporary testimony and cannot be treated as a record of the event. The same feature quotes his later reflection that he learned "the values of respecting facts and working hard with perseverance" from the farmers.

The political consequence is given different emphasis in different profiles. The 2022 profile says he joined the CPC in Liangjiahe and later became village Party chief, the beginning of his political career ([Xi Jinping leads China on new journey](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/25/content_WS6357e5c6c6d0a757729e1c01.html)). A 2023 report is more precise: he joined in January 1974 and became Party branch secretary of the Liangjiahe Brigade later that year ([Xi Jinping unanimously elected Chinese president, 10 March 2023](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202303/10/content_WS640a9f49c6d0a757729e7ee1.html)). The same 2022 profile says that in rural Shaanxi he "devoured literary classics of the world, such as Faust and William Shakespeare's plays", read *Das Kapital* three times and filled 18 notebooks with his reflections ([Xi Jinping leads China on new journey](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/25/content_WS6357e5c6c6d0a757729e1c01.html)). That detail is an intellectual claim made by the profile.

One official vignette includes a named local person. The 2025 feature on women and household work says Xi asked a villager, Wu Hui, why a woman's life was always so hard, and then organised a sewing cooperative so women could earn an income through their skills ([Xi Story: Women breaking free from household chores](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/11/content_WS68e9be87c6d00ca5f9a06aec.html)). It is a small story with a clear purpose in the official narrative, showing early concern for rural women, and it is recorded long after the fact.

## The provincial career

Between the educated youth years and national office, the official biography places study at Tsinghua University from 1975 to 1979 and a posting as secretary to the Minister of National Defence in the late 1970s ([Xi Jinping leads China on new journey](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/25/content_WS6357e5c6c6d0a757729e1c01.html)). In 1982 he volunteered for grassroots work in Zhengding, a poor county in Hebei province. The 2023 profile puts the choice in pointed terms: "While many of his college peers opted to go abroad, Xi applied to work in a poor county called Zhengding in Hebei Province in the early 1980s" ([Profile: With popular mandate, 15 March 2023](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202303/15/content_WS64111103c6d0f528699db48a.html)). He served there as deputy Party chief and then Party chief for three years. The profile describes him riding a bicycle to communes and production teams and joining villagers at work in the fields ([Xi Jinping leads China on new journey](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/25/content_WS6357e5c6c6d0a757729e1c01.html)). A 2026 heritage feature says he worked to secure funds for restoring parts of Longxing Temple, one of China's oldest Buddhist sites ([Preserving the past, bridging civilizations, 13 June 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202606/13/content_WS6a2d3aa1c6d00ca5f9a0b90a.html)). Cultural preservation recurs through the provincial years.

Xi then spent more than seventeen years in Fujian and nearly five in Zhejiang, holding posts as vice mayor of Xiamen, Party chief of Ningde and Fuzhou, provincial governor and provincial Party secretary. A 2025 feature describes Xiamen in 1985 as a newly designated Special Economic Zone whose international trade rested on two aging gantry cranes, a picture of a province at the beginning of its opening to foreign investment ([Xi Focus: Mastering China's long game, 23 October 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/23/content_WS68f991afc6d00ca5f9a06f86.html)). In Ningde he wrote in a 1988 essay that "A weak hatchling bird can be the first to fly, and the poorest can be the first to become rich". A 2026 poverty feature records that he later visited Xiadang, a mountain township without road access, and returned twice to help with roads, electricity and water ([Xi's decades-long commitment to poverty reduction, 17 October 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202610/17/content_WS6ad3135bc6d00ca5f9a0dbe2.html)). In 1990, while Party secretary of Fuzhou, he stopped the demolition of a historic neighbourhood known as Three Lanes and Seven Alleys and ordered its restoration ([Xi Story: Preserving cultural roots, 13 June 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202606/13/content_WS6a2cc797c6d00ca5f9a0b905.html)). In Zhejiang he served as provincial Party secretary from 2002 to 2007, and state media later linked that tenure to the province's designation as a pilot zone for common prosperity ([Xi Jinping leads China on new journey](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/25/content_WS6357e5c6c6d0a757729e1c01.html)).

In 2007 he moved briefly to Shanghai as Party chief before the 17th National Congress elected him to the Standing Committee of the Political Bureau of the CPC Central Committee. This is the Party's top decision making body, not the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress, the state legislature ([Xi Jinping leads China on new journey](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/25/content_WS6357e5c6c6d0a757729e1c01.html)). He served as Vice President of the PRC from 2008 to 2012 and oversaw preparations for the Beijing Olympics and Paralympics. On 15 November 2012, at the First Plenary Session of the 18th CPC Central Committee, he became General Secretary, the first holder of the post born after the founding of the PRC in 1949. He was elected president in March 2013. The years 2012 to 2017 are sometimes called the Xi Li era, a chronological convenience rather than an official term, since Li Keqiang was premier from 2013.

## The 19th Congress and the Thought

The 19th National Congress opened on 18 October 2017. Xi's report, officially titled *Secure a Decisive Victory in Building a Moderately Prosperous Society in All Respects and Strive for the Great Success of Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era*, set out both an account of the previous five years and a doctrine for the future. Its theme called on the party to "remain true to our original aspiration and founding mission" and to "work tirelessly to realize the Chinese Dream of national rejuvenation" ([19th Congress report, official English text](https://web.archive.org/web/20211224030914/http://en.qstheory.cn/2021-12/24/c_692748.htm)). The most quoted passage of the report states:

> "The original aspiration and mission of Chinese Communists is to seek happiness for the Chinese people and rejuvenation for the Chinese nation. This original aspiration, this mission, is what inspires Chinese Communists to advance."

Source: [19th Congress report](https://web.archive.org/web/20211224030914/http://en.qstheory.cn/2021-12/24/c_692748.htm).

The report has thirteen sections, labelled I to XIII, from the review of the previous five years through Party self governance. Its third section sets out fourteen points of basic policy. The fourteen points are not additional sections; they are a list within a section ([State Council Information Office, "13 parts of Xi's report to 19th CPC National Congress", 19 October 2017](http://english.scio.gov.cn/2017-10/19/content_41769624.htm); [official full text of the report](https://www.xinhuanet.com/english/download/Xi_Jinping%27s_report_at_19th_CPC_National_Congress.pdf)).

Among the report's formulations, one defined a change in the country's main problem:

> "As socialism with Chinese characteristics has entered a new era, the principal challenge facing Chinese society has evolved. What we now face is the gap between unbalanced and inadequate development and the ever-growing expectation of the people for a better life."

Source: [19th Congress report](https://web.archive.org/web/20211224030914/http://en.qstheory.cn/2021-12/24/c_692748.htm).

Another defined the new era itself: "With decades of hard work, socialism with Chinese characteristics has crossed the threshold into a new era. This is a new historic juncture in China's development." The report insisted on continuity as well: "The basic dimension of the Chinese context, that our country is still and will long remain in the primary stage of socialism, has not changed. China's international status as the world's largest developing country has not changed" ([19th Congress report](https://web.archive.org/web/20211224030914/http://en.qstheory.cn/2021-12/24/c_692748.htm)).

The Thought itself was summarised in eight statements. One of them says: "It makes clear that the defining feature of socialism with Chinese characteristics is the leadership of the Communist Party of China; the greatest strength of the system of socialism with Chinese characteristics is the leadership of the Communist Party of China; the Party is the highest force for political leadership." The report calls the Thought "the latest achievement in adapting Marxism to the Chinese context" and "a guide to action for all our members and all the Chinese people" ([19th Congress report, section III](https://web.archive.org/web/20211224030914/http://en.qstheory.cn/2021-12/24/c_692748.htm)). On 24 October the Congress amended the Party Constitution accordingly ([CPC Constitution enshrines Xi's thought, 24 October 2017](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/top_news/2017/10/24/content_281475919751341.htm)), and on 25 October Xi was re elected General Secretary ([19th CPC National Congress portal](https://english.www.gov.cn/19thcpccongress)).

How should this concentration of authority be understood? The party presents it as the collective wisdom of the party and the people. Independent scholars have offered different readings. Some argue that Xi centralised power through new institutions after 2012, reversing the habits of shared leadership that had developed after Mao ([an institutional analysis of Xi's centralisation of power, *Journal of Contemporary China*, 2017](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10670564.2016.1245505)). Others find that Xi built a dominant faction within the leadership, promoting clients more successfully than Jiang Zemin or Hu Jintao did ([*The China Quarterly*, 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/china-quarterly/article/from-power-balance-to-dominant-faction-in-xi-jinpings-china/C311C96EB52F5186599E67112E5CB821)). A third body of work treats the Party as a resilient organisation whose discipline and ideology have been rebuilt rather than simply captured by one person ([review essay in the *Journal of Chinese Political Science*, 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11366-020-09665-9)). These are interpretive disagreements about the same institutional facts. None of them can be settled by the Congress report alone.

## The constitutional amendment of 2018 and the Centenary Resolution

On 11 March 2018 the First Session of the Thirteenth National People's Congress amended the PRC Constitution, which had been adopted in 1982 and revised in 1988, 1993, 1999 and 2004 ([official text of the PRC Constitution](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/lawsregulations/201911/20/content_WS5ed8856ec6d0b3f0e9499913.html)). The amendment did two things. It put Xi Jinping Thought into the state Constitution as a guiding theory, on a proposal the CPC Central Committee had made on 19 January 2018 ([Xi thought proposed to be included in Constitution](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/top_news/2018/01/19/content_281476019558364.htm); [Newly elected president takes oath, 17 March 2018](http://english.www.gov.cn/news/top_news/2018/03/17/content_281476080621599.htm)). It also created the constitutional basis for a National Supervisory Commission by merging the Ministry of Supervision into it, a change set out in a new section of Chapter III of the Constitution ([Ministry of Supervision to be merged into new supervisory commission, 13 March 2018](https://english.www.gov.cn/state_council/ministries/2018/03/13/content_281476076512662.htm)). On 17 March Xi was re elected president unanimously and became the first Chinese president to take a public oath of allegiance to the Constitution ([oath report](http://english.www.gov.cn/news/top_news/2018/03/17/content_281476080621599.htm)).

From 8 to 11 November 2021 the Sixth Plenary Session of the 19th CPC Central Committee adopted the *Resolution on the Major Achievements and Historical Experience of the Party over the Past Century*, published in full on 16 November. It is the party's official account of its first hundred years, and it closes with these words:

> "We are convinced that the Communist Party of China and the Chinese people will build upon the great glories and victories of the past hundred years with even greater glories and victories on the new journey that lies before us in the new era."

Source: [Full text of the Centenary Resolution, 16 November 2021](https://english.www.gov.cn/policies/latestreleases/202111/16/content_WS6193a935c6d0df57f98e50b0.html).

The session was attended by 197 members and 151 alternate members of the Central Committee, and Xi delivered an explanatory address that was published the next day ([CPC plenum passes landmark resolution, 11 November 2021](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202111/11/content_WS618d247ac6d0df57f98e4d38.html); [Xi's explanation of the resolution, 17 November 2021](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202111/17/content_WS61945ecbc6d0df57f98e5141.html)). On 1 July 2021, at the centenary ceremony in Tian'anmen Square, Xi announced that the first centenary goal had been achieved and that China had "brought about a historic resolution to the problem of absolute poverty in China" ([Xi declares China a moderately prosperous society, 1 July 2021](http://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202107/01/content_WS60ddd47ec6d0df57f98dc472.html)). That is the official declaration. Its meaning is examined below in the section on poverty.

## The 20th Congress and the plenary sessions

The 20th National Congress opened on 16 October 2022. Xi's report was titled *Hold High the Great Banner of Socialism with Chinese Characteristics and Strive in Unity to Build a Modern Socialist Country in All Respects*. It has three parts and fifteen sections and more than 30,000 Chinese characters ([Report to the 20th Congress: how it was shaped, 28 October 2022](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/28/content_WS63620a10c6d0a757729e22f0.html); [full English text, *Qiushi*](https://en.qstheory.cn/2026-06/10/c_1186537.htm)). Its central task was "Advancing the rejuvenation of the Chinese nation on all fronts through a Chinese path to modernization". The report also named what it called the two establishments: "the establishment of Comrade Xi Jinping's core position on the Party Central Committee and in the Party as a whole and the guiding role of Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era". The Congress said this had set national rejuvenation on "an irreversible historical course" ([Xi expresses confidence as congress concludes, 22 October 2022](http://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/22/content_WS6353d824c6d0a757729e18ae.html)). Xi was re elected General Secretary on 23 October, and on 10 March 2023 he was elected president for a third term with the votes of the 2,952 deputies present ([Xi unanimously elected president, 10 March 2023](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202303/10/content_WS640a9f49c6d0a757729e7ee1.html)). At the same session the National People's Congress amended the Legislation Law, a statutory change rather than a constitutional one ([Profile: With popular mandate, 15 March 2023](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202303/15/content_WS64111103c6d0f528699db48a.html)). Li Qiang succeeded Li Keqiang as premier in March 2023 ([State Council work rules, 24 March 2023](https://english.www.gov.cn/policies/latestreleases/202303/24/content_WS641d5344c6d0f528699dc1f4.html)).

The Third Plenary Session of the 20th Central Committee met from 15 to 18 July 2024 and adopted a resolution on further deepening reform comprehensively to advance Chinese modernisation. It set the goal of finishing a high standard socialist market economy by 2035 and completing its reform tasks by 2029, the eightieth anniversary of the PRC ([communique of the Third Plenum, 18 July 2024](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202407/18/content_WS669906cdc6d0868f4e8e93db.html); [full resolution, 21 July 2024](https://english.www.gov.cn/policies/latestreleases/202407/21/content_WS669d0255c6d0868f4e8e94f8.html)). Official accounts say the drafting took more than seven months, gathered 1,911 suggestions by 30 May and went through 221 revisions ([formulation of the reform resolution, 23 July 2024](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202407/23/content_WS669ee398c6d0868f4e8e9591.html)).

The Fourth Plenary Session met from 20 to 23 October 2025 and adopted recommendations for the Fifteenth Five Year Plan covering 2026 to 2030. Official guidance says the plan carries unusual weight because only ten years remain before 2035, the target date for basically achieving socialist modernisation, and describes the next five years as a window of opportunity ([communique of the Fourth Plenum, 23 October 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/23/content_WS68fa3825c6d00ca5f9a06fbe.html); [official explanation, 31 October 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/31/content_WS6903f4b4c6d00ca5f9a07327.html)). The recommendations set seven objectives, from high quality development and technological self reliance to better public services, environmental goals and national security. Their first priority is a modernised industrial system, including new energy, new materials, aerospace, quantum technology, biomanufacturing, hydrogen and fusion energy, brain computer interfaces and sixth generation mobile communications ([objectives of the recommendations, 24 October 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/24/content_WS68fb10fec6d00ca5f9a06fef.html); [industrial priorities](http://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/24/content_WS68fb1080c6d00ca5f9a06fed.html)).

The Fifth Plenary Session met from 26 to 29 October 2026 and adopted a resolution on full and rigorous Party self governance, stressing what it called a tough stance against corruption and stronger oversight of power ([communique of the Fifth Plenum, 29 October 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202610/29/content_WS6ae348a0c6d00ca5f9a0df80.html)). Official figures issued at the session say that since 2012 discipline inspection bodies have filed 8.105 million cases and punished 7.922 million people, including 963 officials supervised by the CPC Central Committee ([CPC advances Party self governance, 30 October 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202610/30/content_WS6ae4aa49c6d00ca5f9a0dfcf.html)). On 12 January 2026 Xi called for confining power to an institutional cage and said that all are equal before the law and regulations ([Xi urges advancing Party self governance, 12 January 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202601/12/content_WS6964dcc4c6d00ca5f9a0888c.html)).

## The vocabulary of the new era

Official texts rely on a set of compact formulas. Knowing what they mean in plain English makes the rest of the record easier to read.

The Chinese Dream is the foundational slogan. In his first speech after election as president, on 17 March 2013, Xi said that realising a moderately prosperous society and a modern socialist country "means that we will make China prosperous and strong, rejuvenate the nation, and bring happiness to the Chinese people", and that the dream "is, in the final analysis, the dream of the people" ([Address to the First Session of the 12th National People's Congress](https://web.archive.org/web/20260222040419/https://en.qstheory.cn/2020-09/21/c_607581.htm)). His first formulation, on 29 November 2012 during a visit to an exhibition called Road to Rejuvenation, called national rejuvenation "the greatest dream of the Chinese people in the modern era" and set the two centenary timetable: a moderately prosperous society by 2021, the Party's centenary, and a modern socialist country by 2049, the PRC's centenary ([Achieving Rejuvenation Is the Dream of the Chinese People](https://web.archive.org/web/20240707142152/http://en.qstheory.cn/2020-09/18/c_607580.htm)).

The four comprehensives, given official form in 2015, are the goals of building a moderately prosperous society, deepening reform, advancing the rule of law and strictly governing the CPC ([Leaders call for implementation of 'four comprehensives', 4 March 2015](http://english.www.gov.cn/premier/news/2015/03/04/content_281475065652878.htm)). They are often paired with the five sphere integrated plan, meaning economic, political, cultural, social and environmental construction.

The Four Confidences are confidence in the path, theory, system and culture of socialism with Chinese characteristics. The Four Consciousnesses and the Two Upholds are loyalty formulas. A scholarly account defines the Four Consciousnesses as political consciousness, consciousness of the overall situation, consciousness of the core and consciousness of lining up with the leadership; the Two Upholds mean upholding Xi as the core of the Party centre and upholding the authority and central unity of the leadership ([*The China Quarterly*, 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/china-quarterly/article/balances-norms-and-institutions-why-elite-politics-in-the-ccp-have-not-institutionalized/367588D4875D0986929867E78633983B)). Official texts routinely join the formulas together in instructions to Party members ([Xi stresses enhancing Party building, 1 August 2024](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202408/01/content_WS66ab8349c6d0868f4e8e9aa1.html)).

Supply side structural reform means state action to improve the supply side of the economy, for example by closing excess industrial capacity and upgrading technology so that firms produce better goods. The 19th Congress report credited it with "a steady improvement in the economic structure" ([19th Congress report](https://web.archive.org/web/20211224030914/http://en.qstheory.cn/2021-12/24/c_692748.htm)); the Third Plenum of 2024 called for deepening it ([CPC Central Committee adopts reform resolution, 18 July 2024](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202407/18/content_WS6698d829c6d0868f4e8e93d1.html)). Dual circulation is the idea that overseas and domestic markets should reinforce each other, with the domestic market as the mainstay ([poverty alleviation guideline, 23 March 2021](https://english.www.gov.cn/policies/latestreleases/202103/23/content_WS605926e8c6d0719374afb365.html)). New quality productive forces, a phrase defined by Xi in a *Qiushi* article, means advanced productive forces led by innovation, "characterized by high technology, high efficiency, and high quality" and resting on breakthroughs in technology and the upgrading of industry ([New Quality Productive Forces, *Qiushi*](https://en.qstheory.cn/2026-08/05/c_1201934.htm)). He cautioned in 2024 that this did not mean abandoning traditional industries or rushing headlong into new ones ([Leverage Local Conditions to Develop New Quality Productive Forces](https://en.qstheory.cn/2026-08/07/c_1201945.htm)).

Whole-process people's democracy is the party's claim about its own political system. An official text defines it as a democracy that "integrates process-oriented democracy with results-oriented democracy, procedural democracy with substantive democracy, direct democracy with indirect democracy, and people's democracy with the will of the state", and calls it "the broadest, most genuine, and most effective socialist democracy" ([Whole-Process People's Democracy, *The Governance of China* IV](https://web.archive.org/web/20251105064026/https://en.qstheory.cn/2023-07/21/c_904217.htm)). This is a self description. Scholars who study the National Security Law in Hong Kong, discussed below, reach a very different judgement about how much room for contestation the system allows.

Common prosperity means reducing the gaps between urban and rural areas, between regions and between income levels. The 19th Congress report described the new era as one in which people would work together "and ultimately achieve common prosperity for everyone" ([19th Congress report](https://web.archive.org/web/20211224030914/http://en.qstheory.cn/2021-12/24/c_692748.htm)). In 2024 Xi wrote that China would "improve basic public services, narrow the gaps between urban and rural areas, different regions, and different income levels" ([Build a Great Country and Advance National Rejuvenation Through Chinese Modernization](https://en.qstheory.cn/2026-06/29/c_1190590.htm)). Chinese modernisation, another central phrase, means a modernization path which the 20th Congress report says has features common to all countries and features specific to China ([Chinese Modernization Is a Sure Path to Building a Great Country](https://web.archive.org/web/20260108195649/https://en.qstheory.cn/2024-01/05/c_1004960.htm)). Ecological civilisation is the official term for placing environmental protection inside the modernisation programme, expressed in the slogan that "lucid waters and lush mountains are invaluable assets" ([Xi's book on governance helps world better understand China, 25 October 2022](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/25/content_WS6357ce07c6d0a757729e1bfb.html)).

## Poverty, the economy and the claims made for both

The State Council Information Office's white paper of April 2021, *Poverty Alleviation: China's Experience and Contribution*, opens by calling the campaign against poverty "unprecedented in scale and intensity" and says it "benefited the largest number of people in human history". It quotes the declaration of 25 February 2021: "Victory in the battle against poverty is complete, and China completed the arduous task of eliminating extreme poverty" ([official white paper](https://english.www.gov.cn/atts/stream/files/606bc6cac6d0cc300eea77be)). Xinhua reported that the final 98.99 million rural residents classified as impoverished were lifted out of poverty by the end of 2020, and that 770 million people had been lifted out of poverty over more than forty years ([Xi declares China a moderately prosperous society, 1 July 2021](http://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202107/01/content_WS60ddd47ec6d0df57f98dc472.html)). The apparatus described officially involved 255,000 work teams and 3 million cadres sent to villages, with Xi himself conducting more than fifty inspections and visits to all fourteen regions with high concentrations of extreme poverty ([Profile: With popular mandate, 15 March 2023](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202303/15/content_WS64111103c6d0f528699db48a.html)). These are official figures based on China's own poverty line. They measure a defined threshold, not the absence of hardship. A five year transition period after the elimination of absolute poverty was set by a March 2021 guideline on rural revitalisation ([guideline on poverty alleviation outcomes](https://english.www.gov.cn/policies/latestreleases/202103/23/content_WS605926e8c6d0719374afb365.html)), and a 2026 official review described agriculture and rural areas as the foundation of Chinese modernisation ([Xi Focus: Cementing rural foundation, 2 March 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202603/02/content_WS69a4e992c6d00ca5f9a096bd.html)).

Economic policy under Xi is presented officially as a sequence of named stages. The first was the new normal and supply side structural reform; the 19th Congress report spoke of "profound changes as China has entered a new normal in economic development". The second was high quality development and dual circulation, set out in the Fourteenth Five Year Plan. The third was new quality productive forces, institutionalised in the 2024 Third Plenum resolution and listed as a major task in the 2025 Government Work Report ([2025 goals and tasks](https://english.www.gov.cn/policies/featured/202503/07/content_WS67cb05cbc6d0868f4e8f093d.html)). The fourth is the Fifteenth Five Year Plan and its industrial priorities. In December 2025 Xi wrote in *Qiushi* that expanding domestic demand "is not a stopgap but a strategic measure that bears on economic stability and security", and that domestic demand and opening up are not contradictory ([Xi's article on expanding domestic demand, 15 December 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202512/15/content_WS693fd017c6d00ca5f9a0819c.html)). Xinhua's year end review said GDP was expected to grow by about 5 percent in 2025, with output projected at roughly 140 trillion yuan ([Xi leads China through a pivotal 2025, 2 January 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202601/02/content_WS69571dc1c6d00ca5f9a08610.html)). Such numbers are estimates published in an official year end review.

Science and technology feature heavily in the official record. The 19th Congress report listed the Tiangong space lab, the Jiaolong submersible, the FAST telescope, the Wukong and Mozi satellites and the C919 airliner as achievements of the first term ([19th Congress report](https://web.archive.org/web/20211224030914/http://en.qstheory.cn/2021-12/24/c_692748.htm)). The Third Plenum communique called education, science and technology and talent a basic strategic underpinning for modernisation ([communique of the Third Plenum](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202407/18/content_WS669906cdc6d0868f4e8e93db.html)). Under the Fifteenth Five Year Plan recommendations, the Science and Technology Minister set out priorities from 2026 to 2030, including original innovation, the integration of scientific and industrial innovation and the Digital China initiative ([Chinese sci tech minister outlines innovation plans, 24 October 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/24/content_WS68fb6f9bc6d00ca5f9a07016.html)).

## Hong Kong, Xinjiang and Taiwan: official claims and independent findings

Three areas show why official statements must be read alongside other evidence.

**Hong Kong.** The legal framework rests on the PRC Constitution and the Basic Law of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region. On 28 May 2020 the National People's Congress decided to enact national security legislation for Hong Kong, allowing central government security organs to set up agencies in the territory ([China adopts decision to make Hong Kong national security laws](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202005/28/content_WS5ecf67d3c6d0b3f0e9498dc7.html)). The resulting law, enacted on 30 June 2020, created offences of secession, subversion, terrorism and collusion with foreign forces. An official statement said the law "fully complies with the 'one country, two systems' principle" and was "entirely an internal affair of China" ([HKSAR national security law to boost one country, two systems, 1 July 2020](https://english.www.gov.cn/statecouncil/ministries/202007/01/content_WS5efc443bc6d05a0f897067ba.html)). The government later described "patriots administering Hong Kong" as the key to improving the electoral system ([State Council Hong Kong and Macao Affairs Office, 11 March 2021](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202103/11/content_WS604a0ef1c6d0719374afaa5b.html)), and electoral changes in 2021 cut the number of directly elected Legislative Council seats to twenty out of ninety ([Asia Policy, 2021](https://www.nbr.org/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/publications/ap16-2_davis_apr2021.pdf)).

A State Council Information Office white paper of 20 December 2021 stated: "There was no democracy in Hong Kong under the British rule before the city returned to China in 1997" ([white paper on democracy in Hong Kong](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/videos/202112/20/content_WS61c03c5cc6d09c94e48a27c3.html)). The Legislative Council's own published history contradicts that statement. Direct elections to the Legislative Council began in 1991 with eighteen seats, and a fully elected Council of sixty members was formed in 1995 ([Hong Kong Legislative Council, historical development of the legislature](https://www.legco.gov.hk/en/education/understand/timeline.html); [research paper on political development since the 1980s](https://www.legco.gov.hk/general/english/procedur/companion/chapter_3/mcp-part1-ch3-n1-e.pdf)). The white paper's claim is therefore a contested political assertion, not a neutral description. Independent legal scholars have argued that the National Security Law weakened the rule of law and self government in Hong Kong by expanding state coercive powers, limiting judicial scrutiny and allowing mainland jurisdiction in some cases ([Hong Kong's National Security Law and the struggle over rule of law and democracy, 2022](https://web.archive.org/web/20230427022558/https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0067205X221107410); [Georgetown Law Asia report, 2021](https://www.law.georgetown.edu/law-asia/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2021/02/GT-HK-Report-Accessible.pdf)). A 2026 white paper described the territory's transition from disorder to stability and prosperity ([Hong Kong national security white paper, 10 February 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202602/10/content_WS698ae458c6d00ca5f9a0903e.html)). Whether Hong Kong has been stabilised at the cost of its earlier political freedoms is a question on which the official and independent accounts give opposite answers.

**Xinjiang.** A series of State Council Information Office white papers set out the official position. *Vocational Education and Training in Xinjiang* of 17 August 2019 described centres established to prevent terrorism and religious extremism and said trainees' basic rights were protected ([official text](http://english.www.gov.cn/archive/whitepaper/201908/17/content_WS5d57573cc6d0c6695ff7ed6c.html)). *Respecting and Protecting the Rights of All Ethnic Groups in Xinjiang* of July 2021 stated: "For more than four years since the end of 2016 there has been no terrorist incident in Xinjiang. The infiltration of extremism has been effectively curbed, and the right to life of people of all ethnic groups has been fully protected" ([official text](http://english.www.gov.cn/archive/whitepaper/202107/14/content_WS60ee599bc6d0df57f98dcd8c.html)). An earlier white paper on employment and labour rights said that 2.92 million people from 737,600 households had escaped poverty between 2014 and the end of 2019 ([Employment and Labor Rights in Xinjiang, 17 September 2020](http://english.www.gov.cn/archive/whitepaper/202009/17/content_WS5f62cef6c6d0f7257693c192.html)).

These are claims made by the state. The Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights published an assessment on 31 August 2022 which reached a different conclusion. Drawing on official documents, open source material and forty in depth interviews with people with direct knowledge, including twenty six who said they had been detained or had worked in the facilities, OHCHR concluded that a pattern of large scale arbitrary detention had occurred in vocational education and training centres, at least between 2017 and 2019. It found that placement was not voluntary, that interviewees were not told how long they would stay, and that two thirds of former detainees it interviewed described treatment amounting to torture or ill treatment, including beatings with electric batons and prolonged solitary confinement. One interviewee described being made to sing patriotic songs until his face reddened. OHCHR said it could not confirm a total number of detainees but judged the system to have operated on a wide scale across the region ([OHCHR assessment, 31 August 2022](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/countries/2022-08-31/22-08-31-final-assesment.pdf); [UN News summary, 1 September 2022](https://news.un.org/en/story/2022/08/1125932)). China rejected the assessment. The two accounts disagree about both the character of the centres and their scale, and the disagreement cannot be resolved from official white papers alone.

**Taiwan.** The white paper *The Taiwan Question and China's Reunification in the New Era*, issued on 10 August 2022, states: "We are one China, and Taiwan is part of China. This is an indisputable fact supported by history and the law. Taiwan has never been a state; its status as part of China is unalterable." It invokes UN General Assembly Resolution 2758 as a political document with unquestioned legal authority ([Taiwan white paper, 10 August 2022](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/whitepaper/202208/10/content_WS62f34f46c6d02e533532f0ac.html)). The 20th Congress report said that force would never be renounced and that the party reserved the option of all necessary measures ([Xi: CPC to unswervingly advance national reunification, 16 October 2022](http://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202210/16/content_WS634b89a1c6d0a757729e1493.html)). The 19th Congress report had pledged that "We will never allow anyone, any organization, or any political party, at any time or in any form, to separate any part of Chinese territory from China" ([19th Congress report, section XII](https://web.archive.org/web/20211224030914/http://en.qstheory.cn/2021-12/24/c_692748.htm)). The status of Taiwan and the meaning of Resolution 2758 remain disputed in international legal and political debate; the white paper states one side of that dispute.

## COVID-19: official management and its costs

Official accounts present COVID-19 policy as a series of adjustments grounded in the stated aims of protecting life and limiting disruption. In November 2022 a meeting of the Standing Committee of the Political Bureau, chaired by Xi, set out the formula of "preventing both imported cases and domestic resurgences" and "dynamic zero-COVID", meaning a policy of stamping out outbreaks through mass testing, contact tracing and lockdowns ([Standing Committee meeting report, 11 November 2022](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202211/11/content_WS636e27a2c6d0a757729e2e60.html)). The same meeting noted that China had many vulnerable groups and uneven medical resources. On 11 November the State Council issued twenty measures which stressed that "Optimizing and adjusting the COVID-19 response measures does not mean loosening prevention and control against the virus, still less a lift of COVID-19 restrictions or 'lying flat' in the COVID fight" ([China releases measures to optimise COVID response](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202211/11/content_WS636e31efc6d0a757729e2e63.html)). Ten further measures followed on 7 December 2022, shifting the stated focus from preventing infection to preventing severe cases ([10 new measures, 8 December 2022](https://english.www.gov.cn/statecouncil/ministries/202212/08/content_WS63913c92c6d0a757729e4135.html)). From 8 January 2023 COVID-19 was downgraded from Class A management to Class B management, ending mass nucleic acid testing and centralised quarantine ([China strengthens COVID-19 response for key groups, 27 December 2022](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202212/27/content_WS63aae802c6d0a757729e4d37.html); [Optimized COVID response speeds up recovery, 5 January 2023](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/topnews/202301/05/content_WS63b67a50c6d0a757729e5220.html)). Official framing described the change as science based and aimed at restoring normal life ([Foreign Ministry explanation, 29 December 2022](http://english.www.gov.cn/statecouncil/ministries/202212/29/content_WS63acd911c6d0a757729e4db3.html)).

The independent record adds what that framing leaves out. *The Lancet* reported in May 2022 that Shanghai's lockdown separated families, caused food shortages and disrupted access to medical care. A Shanghai resident described his father being unable to get dialysis without a negative PCR test, and a public health academic at Yale said the focus on COVID had undercounted other diseases ([Zero COVID in China: what next?, *The Lancet*, 14 May 2022](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2822%2900873-X/fulltext)). A modelling study published in 2023 estimated the social cost of Shanghai's first Omicron wave at 415 billion yuan, with movement restrictions accounting for most of it, while noting that its own figures omitted long term effects such as delayed treatment for chronic illness ([cost effectiveness analysis, *Journal of Infection and Public Health*, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10085878/)). In late November 2022 young people, including students and graduates, held protests in several cities, many holding blank sheets of paper; a study of the policy argues that these protests contributed to the abrupt reversal in December ([Zero-COVID Policy in China: Epidemic Control and the Mode of Politics, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1142/s1793930523000296)). The policy reduced transmission for two years, and some researchers defend it on those grounds ([The end of zero COVID-19 policy is not the end of COVID-19 for China, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanwpc.2023.100702)). The open questions concern the price paid in other illness, livelihoods and public confidence, and how much of that price was unavoidable.

## Foreign policy and the global initiatives

The Belt and Road Initiative was announced during visits to Central Asia and South East Asia in September and October 2013. The foundational action plan of March 2015 set out five cooperation priorities: policy coordination, infrastructure connectivity, unimpeded trade, financial integration and people to people bonds. It described peace and cooperation, openness and inclusiveness, mutual learning and mutual benefit as the Silk Road Spirit and said this spirit had been handed down through generations ([Vision and Actions on Jointly Building Silk Road Economic Belt and 21st Century Maritime Silk Road, March 2015](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/publications/2015/03/30/content_281475080041500.htm)). A maritime vision of 2017 added a Blue Partnership and described sea passages linking the Indian Ocean, Africa, the Mediterranean, Oceania and the Arctic ([Vision for Maritime Cooperation, 20 June 2017](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/publications/2017/06/20/content_281475691873460.htm)). A 2023 white paper called the initiative "a public road open to all, not a private path owned by any single party" and said it was free from geopolitical calculation ([BRI white paper coverage, 11 October 2023](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202310/11/content_WS6525f80ac6d0868f4e8e0200.html)). Independent assessments of the initiative's debt and environmental effects are not considered in that white paper.

Official sources list four global initiatives. The Global Development Initiative was proposed in 2021, the Global Security Initiative in 2022 and the Global Civilizations Initiative in 2023. The fourth, the Global Governance Initiative, was announced at the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Tianjin on 1 September 2025. Xi set out five principles: sovereign equality, international rule of law, multilateralism, a people centred approach and real action ([Xi proposes Global Governance Initiative, 1 September 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202509/01/content_WS68b58afbc6d0868f4e8f53cd.html); [Four Global Initiatives, *Qiushi*, 2 November 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20261106222106/https://en.qstheory.cn/2026-11/02/c_1223043.htm)). He said that "Global governance has come to a new crossroads" and that "The house rules of a few countries must not be imposed upon others". The official summary says the four initiatives aim at shared growth, cooperative security, mutual learning among civilisations and a more just global governance system ([Xi's article on promoting implementation of global initiatives, 15 October 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202510/15/content_WS68ef601cc6d00ca5f9a06d2e.html)). Chinese sources say the initiatives have drawn support from nearly 160 countries and international organisations ([*Qiushi*, 2 November 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20261106222106/https://en.qstheory.cn/2026-11/02/c_1223043.htm)); that is a claim about diplomatic reception made by the originating state.

In other diplomatic fields, the 19th Congress report recorded China's hosting of the G20 summit in Hangzhou in 2016 and the BRICS summit in Xiamen, as well as its role in creating the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank and the Silk Road Fund ([19th Congress report](https://web.archive.org/web/20211224030914/http://en.qstheory.cn/2021-12/24/c_692748.htm)). In May 2025 Xi called on China and Russia to maintain strategic coordination "as the world is entering a new period of turbulence and transformation" ([China, Russia to maintain strategic resolve, 9 May 2025](http://english.www.gov.cn/news/202505/09/content_WS681d3938c6d0868f4e8f25c2.html)). Xinhua's diplomatic review for 2025 recorded a large military parade in Beijing on 3 September 2025, two days after the SCO summit in Tianjin ([Xi's diplomacy in 2025, 30 December 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202512/30/content_WS695323d7c6d00ca5f9a08515.html)). At the eighteenth BRICS summit in September 2026 Xi presented the four initiatives as a common programme for a more just international order ([full text of the BRICS speech, 13 September 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202609/13/content_WS6aa6203ac6d00ca5f9a0d2b0.html)).

## Party building and the anti corruption campaign

The anti corruption campaign is one of the defining features of the period, and it attracts sharply different interpretations. The 19th Congress report said the party had adopted Eight Rules on conduct, acted against "formalism, bureaucratism, hedonism, and extravagance", and fought corruption without exemptions: "No place has been out of bounds, no ground left unturned, and no tolerance shown in the fight against corruption" ([19th Congress report](https://web.archive.org/web/20211224030914/http://en.qstheory.cn/2021-12/24/c_692748.htm)). Xi's own formulation in January 2020 was to catch "tigers" as well as "flies", meaning senior officials as well as low level ones, and to focus on violations committed after the 18th Congress ([Strengthen Discipline and Scrutiny over the Exercise of Power, *The Governance of China* III](https://en.qstheory.cn/2022-08/12/c_1222027.htm)). A 2025 collection named Zhou Yongkang, Bo Xilai, Guo Boxiong, Xu Caihou and Ling Jihua among officials removed ([Strengthen Internal Party Activities and Governance](https://en.qstheory.cn/2025-05/07/c_1222005.htm)). Xi warned in 2012 that unchecked corruption "could spell the end of both the Party and the state" ([Xi leads CPC to advance full rigorous self governance, 26 October 2026](http://english.www.gov.cn/news/202610/26/content_WS6adf676bc6d00ca5f9a0df2e.html)).

The institutional change of 2018, which created the National Supervisory Commission, gave the drive a permanent legal base. The Fifth Plenum of October 2026 added a dedicated resolution on self governance covering conduct, discipline, anti corruption work, Party regulations and oversight systems ([CPC advances Party self governance, 30 October 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202610/30/content_WS6ae4aa49c6d00ca5f9a0dfcf.html)).

Scholars differ over what the campaign is for. One reading sees it as a genuine effort to repair the Party's organisational weaknesses and discipline problems. Another sees its main effect as the consolidation of Xi's personal authority, achieved by removing rivals and by expanding the investigative powers of the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection. A review of the literature in the *Journal of Chinese Political Science* concludes that both readings capture part of the campaign and that neither should be treated as the whole explanation ([power struggle or strengthening the Party, *Journal of Chinese Political Science*, 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11366-020-09665-9)). Studies of elite politics give more weight to the centralisation thesis, noting that Xi broke with earlier norms by pursuing retired as well as serving officials and by promoting associates to key posts ([*The China Quarterly*, 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/china-quarterly/article/balances-norms-and-institutions-why-elite-politics-in-the-ccp-have-not-institutionalized/367588D4875D0986929867E78633983B)). The official figures for cases and punishments, however precise they appear, do not by themselves settle the question of purpose.

## The years 2025 and 2026 in official accounts

Official reporting for these years emphasises continuity. On 1 July 2026 Xinhua marked the CPC's 105th anniversary with a report in which Xi urged the building of a modern socialist China ([Xi hails CPC's 105 year epic, 1 July 2026](http://english.www.gov.cn/news/202607/01/content_WS6a4526f8c6d00ca5f9a0bf95.html)). On 30 September 2026 he spoke at a reception for the seventy seventh anniversary of the PRC ([Xi calls for firm confidence, solid steps, 30 September 2026](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202609/30/content_WS6abcf464c6d00ca5f9a0d8cf.html)). The fifth volume of *The Governance of China* appeared in July 2025 and in eight further languages in May 2026 ([fifth volume published, 30 July 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202507/30/content_WS68897fcec6d0868f4e8f48ac.html); [more languages, 25 May 2026](http://english.www.gov.cn/news/202605/25/content_WS6a13d7bbc6d00ca5f9a0b395.html)); by late 2025 the series had been translated into 44 languages and distributed in more than 180 countries and regions ([Greek edition launched, 20 November 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202511/20/content_WS691e51dec6d00ca5f9a07ae3.html)). The Central Economic Work Conference of December 2025 set priorities including more proactive macroeconomic policy, expanded domestic demand, new quality productive forces and a unified national market ([Navigating headwinds, 13 December 2025](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202512/13/content_WS693d6652c6d00ca5f9a08116.html)).

The official record at the end of 2026 therefore describes a party completing one five year plan and starting another, continuing a broad anti corruption drive and presenting four global initiatives as its contribution to world order. Those are the lines the documents set out. How far each is carried out, and with what effects on the people living under it, requires evidence beyond the documents themselves. The chapters on Chinese constitutional arrangements and on China's revolutionary history provide the longer background against which these claims can be judged.
