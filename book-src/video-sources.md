This chapter is a directory of video and sound material produced by the movements and states that made it, or hosted by their own organisations. A recording counts as the speaker's own when the speaker, the organisation in whose name it was made, or a movement archive that holds the figure's recorded material distributes it. This includes the official site of a party or state body and a movement's own film or audio unit. It also includes state broadcasters that host their own material. The directory does not rank films or recommend documentaries for their cinematic merit. It records what exists, topic by topic and figure by figure, and sets that material apart from recordings made by news agencies and by other people.

An official website may show a video from another site inside its own page, a practice called embedding. Such a video remains a recording made by whoever uploaded it, even when a party or museum page displays it. Where a speech survives only on audio, the directory lists it as audio. Where no official source is available, the entry says so.

The material is spread unevenly. Marx and Engels left none, because they died before sound recording and film were in use. Lenin survives in a silent film compilation held by the Marxists Internet Archive (MIA), a volunteer-run library of Marxist writing and related material. Mao's founding ceremony is held by China Central Television, the state broadcaster. The fullest corpora are the official Fidel Castro archive, which organises dozens of indexed pages of video, and the official Thomas Sankara site, which separates audio, video, film and documentary material into distinct sections. The written primary sources remain the foundation of the book; audiovisual material supplements them.

## Founders and classical Marxism

### Marx and Engels: no audiovisual source exists

Karl Marx, who lived from 1818 to 1883, and Friedrich Engels, who lived from 1820 to 1895, died before sound recording was commercially available and long before film production ([MIA Encyclopedia, "Marx, Karl"](https://www.marxists.org/glossary/people/m/a.htm#marx-karl); ["Engels, Friedrich"](https://www.marxists.org/glossary/people/e/n.htm#engels-friedrich)). There is no film of either speaking and no recorded voice. Later films portray them as dramatizations. The book's treatment rests on their written texts: the *Manuscripts of 1844*, the *Theses on Feuerbach*, the *Communist Manifesto*, the *1859 Preface*, *Capital*, *Anti-Dühring*, *Ludwig Feuerbach*, the letters and the MIA's editorial introductory notes. The absence of audiovisual material reflects the history of the medium.

### Lenin: a movement archive hosts a silent film compilation

The MIA's Lenin photograph archive contains a small "VIDEO" section with direct file links. One, labelled "VIDEO of Lenin speaking at various places", links to a film file on the archive's own domain. The archive hosts a silent film compilation of Lenin speaking. Its own index provides the attribution, so there is no need to borrow a title from a commercial source.

The MIA also preserves the facts about Lenin's recorded speeches in its text archive. The introductory note to *Anti-Jewish Pogroms* records that "13 of Lenin's speeches were recorded" on gramophone records in 1919 to 1921 by Tsentropechat. The archive publishes the transcripts, not the recordings. Those transcripts are written primary sources and are not audiovisual material.

### Mao Zedong and the 1949 founding ceremony: official CCTV footage

The clearest audiovisual item for the founders is the founding ceremony of the People's Republic itself. China Central Television (CCTV), the state broadcaster, hosts a clip titled 《1949年10月1日 中华人民共和国开国大典》, "1 October 1949: The Founding Ceremony of the People's Republic of China", labelled on the page 视频来源：央视历史资料, "video source: CCTV historical materials". The description follows Mao and Zhu De up the western stair of the Tiananmen gatehouse, the taking of their places as Lin Boqu declares the meeting open, and Mao's proclamation: "中华人民共和国中央人民政府成立了!" The page carries the date 2010-01-12 and a full description. CCTV is both the state broadcaster and the archive holder.

A second CCTV page, the special 开国大典——1949年大阅兵, "Founding Ceremony, the 1949 military parade", is the official broadcaster's own special page for the same event. A Ministry of Foreign Affairs feature, 《开国大典的彩色胶片》, "The colour film of the founding ceremony", explains the fate of the Soviet-shot colour material and is hosted on the foreign ministry's own website. It is an article with photographs and references to film, not a video file.

## Revolutionary figures

### Thomas Sankara: an extensive official corpus

The official site **thomassankara.net** is the movement's own archive. Its menu has separate sections for audio documents, video documents, films and documentaries. Four kinds of material can be distinguished.

First, state-broadcaster footage preserved on the official site. The page on the 18 October 1984 Poura gold-mine ceremony states that the material was received from the *service des archives de la radio et de la télévision burkinabè*, the Burkinabè radio and television archive. It offers an audio file, a video excerpt from RTB, the Burkinabè state broadcaster, and a transcription prepared by the site. The film is the state broadcaster's own report, preserved by the movement's own site. Second, direct video files on the official site, such as *La gestion foncière sous la Révolution* and *Bilan de l'an I de la Révolution sur le plan foncier*, served from the site's own domain. Third, the site's own YouTube account, `sitethomassankaranet`, which carries the 21 April 1982 speech "Malheur à ceux qui bâillonnent le peuple". Fourth, the official state broadcaster RTB's own channel, which carries the history of Sankara and Gaoua and a film on bean cultivation and Naganagani.

Some official-site pages show video uploaded by private users, including the 4 August 1987 Dailymotion upload and the 4 August 1986 YouTube upload from "TERRE DES ANES sas". Those are third-party recordings. The same applies to a Swedish television report, a private travel-vlogger's account and a private compilation of two speeches.

### The Black Panther Party: movement archives rather than a party website

No stable website of the historic Black Panther Party hosts the Party's filmed or recorded material. What exists is held by movement archives and institutional partners. Freedom Archives describes itself as a working group of "the original radio producers", "radical activists, writers, educators, archivists, historians, and technical producers", holding more than 10,000 hours of audio and video documenting the movements themselves. Its sample pages offer direct downloads of Fred Hampton and Assata Shakur and a curated page, **Malcolm X Speaks**, whose text says the reels were unmarked and that the archive chose clips in which Malcolm directly links African liberation, colonialism and racism.

Pacifica Radio Archives states that its Black Panther Party collection documents "speeches, interviews, news coverage and documentaries by or about the Black Panther Party" and was made with UC Berkeley's Moffitt Library. The archive is run by a movement and says where its recordings came from; it is not a TV network looking back at the past. Pacifica is a listener-supported radio foundation, and the recordings were made on Pacifica stations. The UC Berkeley and Social Activism Sound Recording Project holds catalogue records for the 26 January 1968 Oakland Seven rally, with Bobby Seale, and the 12 November 1969 Bobby Hutton Park rally, with Angela Davis.

### Nkrumah, Lumumba, Cabral and Rodney

These four cases show the difference between an official page and a recording made by the speaker. The Kwame Nkrumah Memorial Park is an official Ghanaian state museum, and its website has a Videos page and a Speeches of Osagyefo Dr. Kwame Nkrumah page. The videos shown on those pages lead to TV3 Ghana, Talking Africa and NewAfrica, a commercial network and two private commentary channels. The text of Patrice Lumumba's 30 June 1960 independence speech is at the MIA. The widely circulated clips are private uploads, a Belgian museum exhibition or unattributed copies. The official **Fundação Amílcar Cabral** site, `amilcarcabral.cv`, has a Galeria, an Arquivo and a statement on films about the liberation struggle; the foundation's social-media video posts are official-account material. The Walter Rodney Foundation's own page states that the Rodney Papers at the Atlanta University Center contain "audio recordings of Walter Rodney lecturing at universities, conferences, and rallies, including his last public speech given at a rally". The recordings are held in a reading room, not offered as playable online files.

## Imperialism and anti-colonialism

The audiovisual material for this topic is uneven. Where a liberation movement had its own broadcaster or archive, there is audio and film. Where it did not, the record is written or survives only in archives run by opponents, and the directory says so. The strongest items are the following.

The official Sankara site publishes the full text of the 4 October 1984 General Assembly speech and has a separate page recording that an Italian-language subtitled video of the speech was published by the Italian Communist Refoundation Party's own channel. Malcolm X's two Freedom Archives reels, *On African Freedom Fighters* and *On Pan-Africanism*, were chosen by the archive precisely because they connect colonial oppression abroad and racist oppression in the United States. Cuba's official party output, Granma's *Conquistando un sueño* series and its video on the "ideario fidelista", documents how the party itself narrates its congresses and its anti-imperialist programme. The Communist Party of Britain's CommieCast is described on the party's own site as "features/interviews on peace, internationalism, housing, education, transport, community building … the state of health and Progressive Federalism", and is hosted on the party's own channel. CPUSA's Good Morning Revolution is a weekly party programme with named party leaders and the party's own stream links, listed again below among contemporary parties.
