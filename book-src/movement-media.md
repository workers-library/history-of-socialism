Movement media are the podcasts, journals, websites, presses and video channels that socialist and communist organisations run themselves. This chapter records projects that describe themselves as Marxist, Marxist-Leninist, communist or socialist. The word outlet is used here to mean a publication, channel or press. Each project is presented through its own about page, published articles, episode pages, catalogue, official social links, or information in its own header and settings. Self-descriptions are quoted or closely paraphrased, and are reported as claims the outlet makes about itself.

The projects described below assign themselves four overlapping functions: teaching theory, preserving historical documents, communicating within an organisation, and expressing solidarity with movements elsewhere. Several outlets attack each other in print, and those disputes remain unresolved; they are recorded here only as the outlets' own positions. A social-media link proves that an outlet chose to publish there. It does not prove the outlet's size or the truth of its claims.

## What counts as movement media

The chapter covers podcasts, video channels, independent news and analysis websites, periodicals, book publishers and printers, zine and newsletter projects, and documentary-style video operations. It also includes, briefly, official party and state publications. The dividing line is not always sharp. A podcast may be published as a video, a publisher may run a video channel, and a party newspaper may host a film unit. The sections below group projects by their main form, and note where the forms overlap.

### The Revolutionary Communist Party's two podcasts

The Revolutionary Communist Party's own podcast page states that Marxist Voice is "the theory and education podcast of Revolutionary Communist Party, bringing you weekly talks and discussions on Marxist theory and revolutionary history." The same page directs listeners who want "our current events podcast" to *A World on Fire*. It also recommends the RCI's *Spectre of Communism* and *Against the Stream*. The page lists subscribers and presenters by name in its own episode entries, including Sarah Vedrovitch, Georgina Ryan, Khaled Malachi, Nelson Wan, Will Collins, Lubna Badi, Josh Holroyd, Tom Crozier, Fred Weston, Rob Sewell and James Kilby. It states: "You can subscribe to our podcast on YouTube, Spotify, Apple Podcasts, Amazon Music, and other major podcast platforms. You can access our RSS feed here." The pair clearly separates theory from news commentary.

The RCP's second podcast is introduced on its own page with the words: "It's time to get real – and to get revolutionary. The Gen-Z revolution is underway, the world is on fire, and the old system is crumbling. In our new show, A World On Fire, the Revolutionary Communist Party will follow this process as it unfolds across Britain and the globe, exposing what the bourgeois media hides and fears." The page adds: "If you're looking for our education and theory podcast, head to Marxist Voice," and "Subscribe on Spotify and YouTube." Episode titles in the site's own listing include "The Future MUST Be Communist" (#19), "COMMUNISM VS LIBERALISM: How to fight racism" (#15), and "Communists discuss: Who REALLY runs Britain?!" (#17).

### The Revolutionary Communist International's podcasts

The Revolutionary Communist International hosts its podcasts at its own [Marxist.com podcasts](https://podcast.marxist.com/) site. The pages describe the presenters in the text of their episode pages. Hamid Alizadeh and Jorge Martín are described as "from the International Secretariat of the Revolutionary Communist International". Other presenters named include Alan Woods, Fred Weston, Marie Frederiksen, described as "leading member of the Revolutionary Communist Party in Denmark", Rob Sewell and Josh Holroyd.

A January 2025 *Against the Stream* episode states that the programme "break[s] down the news of the world from a Marxist perspective" and that "Against the Stream is live on YouTube every Thursday at 3pm GMT." A December 2024 joint season finale explains that "the two podcasts of the RCI, Spectre of Communism and Against the Stream, have come together" to review "a year of war and revolution." It says that in 2024 the RCI launched "a new Revolutionary Communist International… the embryo of the world party of communist revolution so desperately needed by the working class!"

The *Spectre of Communism* episode "Lenin's crusade against imperialist war, lessons for today" points listeners to Wellred Books, "the publishing house of the Revolutionary Communist International", which had "recently published a collection of Lenin's writings on imperialism and war." That is a podcast advertising a publisher in the same movement.

### School of Communism

Also hosted on the RCI's own podcast domain, this series is described as recordings "of all of the speeches from the founding conference of the RCI", held in June 2024 in Italy with thousands participating online. The series page states: "In addition to officially establishing our new International, the conference was a school of communism, with around 20 sessions on all aspects of our theory, history and thought… Together, these talks represent the complete programme of our international." Episode descriptions include "Building the revolutionary party" and "How can the communists conquer the masses?", the latter explaining that "Communists place a great deal of importance on education and theoretical study."

### CommieCast and the People's School

The Communist Party of Britain's site states: "CommieCast is the podcast of Britain's Communist Party. The Communist Party is the Marxist Leninist Party of Britain's labour movement, active in your community, campus and workplace. We've been fighting for peace, democracy and socialism in Britain for more than a century." It continues: "CommieCast is our way of getting across the Communist Party's perspective on domestic and international news and developments as well as being a tool for Marxist education. We have episodes covering news, theory, history, in-depth specials and much more." The episode list includes "Episode #4: The new Cold War against China, Part 1", "Epsiode #2: The Challenge of Anti-Communism in 2024 [sic]", "Not One Step Back from the Truth: Fighting Historical Revisionism", "Ukraine and China: The Quiet American Wars", "Venezuela: a Crisis of Capitalism? A view from the Communist Party of Venezuela", and "Classics of Communism: Socialism and Man in Cuba by Ernesto Che Guevara". The last is credited on Spotify to Johnnie Hunter, with the text credited on the episode page to the Marxists Internet Archive.

The People's School for Marxist-Leninist Studies carries the tagline "Continuing the Long Line of Party-Sponsored Marxist-Leninist Education". Its "About us" section states: "The Peoples School for Marxist-Leninist Studies is part of a long line of American communist party sponsored schools, such as the Workers Schools, the Jefferson School of Social Science, the Center for Marxist Education and the Peoples School for Marxist Studies. We were founded in 2016… We hold classes online every week on Tuesdays at 8 pm EST/5 pm PST. These are public classes on Zoom that are organized into sections of presentations, and discussion periods. Join our classes and participate in collective education! The classes cost nothing and the knowledge is worth everything!" The site's Twitter embedding describes it as "Sponsored by the PCUSA ☭."

Class titles listed on the home page include "The Perils of Fascism Part 2", "Industrial Unionism by William Z. Foster", "Elizabeth Gurley Flynn: Rebel Girl", "The My Lai Massacre", "Bread and Roses Strike", "Women Against Fascism", "What is Socialism" and "Selected Works of Henry Winston." The site links to YouTube, Spotify and SoundCloud. It is both an audio project and a live, video-enabled educational organisation. The home page instructs participants to "PLEASE CALL IN IF YOU ARE UNABLE TO HAVE WEBCAM ON" and explains that "You will be asked to start your video and add your name and state as a security measure."

### Red Flag Radio, Revolutionary Ideas and Better Off Red

The Australian [Red Flag](https://redflag.org.au/) site states on its podcast page: "We are a socialist podcast based in Australia. We discuss politics, theory and history from a revolutionary Marxist perspective. We aim to explain socialist ideas in a clear and accessible way and to promote the voices of anti-capitalist activists worldwide." Listed episodes include "Why Stalinism should stay dead", described in the site's own copy as addressing the claim that "Stalinism equates socialism/communism with state-controlled industry and a one-party dictatorship". Other episodes include "Fighting One Nation's far-right insurgency", "Victorian Socialists election campaign launches! (with Jasmine Duff)" and "The European youth revolts, banning Rising Tide and more." The site also publishes a fortnightly print journal: "New editions of Red Flag are released fortnightly."

Socialist Alternative's own podcast page titles the series "Revolutionary Ideas Podcast" and shows an episode titled "Revolutionary Ideas, A Marxist Podcast, 21: The Militant Tendency, Marxism in the 1980s." Its about page states: "Socialist Alternative is a revolutionary organisation in England, Wales and Scotland, fighting to transform society… That is why we are part of International Socialist Alternative, fighting for a socialist world." The programme includes "public ownership of the major banks and monopolies" and "democratic workers' control and management."

The archived SocialistWorker.org site lists a "Better Off Red Podcast" section with episodes from 2018 and 2019, including "45: Women in tech talk organizing", "43: Wyomia Tyus takes no prisoners; socialism is coming to the USA", "41: LA Teachers' Strike; Helen Scott on Rosa Luxemburg's Legacy" and "31: Building fighting unions at UPS and Delta." The site states that it "was the publication of the International Socialist Organization (U.S.) from the group's founding in April 1977 until its dissolution in 2019", and that it sought "presenting a Marxist analysis of important questions of the day, socialist analysis of world news and events, and keeping alive the rich and too-often-hidden history of working-class struggle and the socialist tradition." The podcast is an archive: the site says "Though SW has stopped publishing, its archives of paper editions dating back to 1977 and its website dating back to 2001 are available at this website and the Marxists Internet Archives."

### Party channels, Green Left and publishers as video producers

Green Left's own about page states: "Green Left sides with the 99%… Our goal is ecosocialism, where society is run by and for ordinary people, in harmony with nature." Its [videos index](https://www.greenleft.org.au/videos) carries original video reports such as "Video: Blockade the schools, French student activist speaks out", "Video: How Palestine solidarity activists are being silenced" and "Video: Newcastle says no to transition from coal to weapons", as well as the interview series "Green Left Show".

Tempest describes itself on its [about page](https://tempestmag.org/about/) as "A revolutionary socialist organizing project" and "an organizing and educational project" committed to "revolutionary socialism from below." Its site has a "Videos and panels" section and links to the "Tempest Mag" YouTube channel. Its own interview with David Camfield about his podcast *Victor's Children*, published on 24 April 2025, gives a reason why a socialist media project chooses the podcast medium: "The political reason I started it was a concern with the weak presence on the Left of the kind of socialism from below politics I support, and because there wasn't an English-language podcast promoting them." The same interview states that "too few supporters of socialist politics that are neither social democratic/'democratic socialist' nor Marxist-Leninist are making podcasts and producing content on YouTube and other platforms where lots of radicalizing people go for political ideas." That interview is cited here as Tempest's own published content, not as an outside assessment of other outlets.

Verso's own about page links to "Verso Books, YouTube". Haymarket's about page describes "the popular Haymarket Books Live event series and podcast" and links to "Haymarket Books, YouTube". Haymarket's menu includes "Videos", "Video Series", "Audio" and "Podcast Series" under Resources. These publishers and periodicals maintain their own audiovisual material on their own domains.

CPUSA's "Party Voices > Videos" feed carries the weekly "GMR" series. Entries include "GMR: Can fascism be defeated without breaking the two party trap?" (17 November 2026), "GMR: Is MAGA's midterm defeat socialism's moment?" and "GMR: Capitalism, Communism & Free Speech", described on the feed as asking "what's it like in socialist China?" Each entry is attributed on the page to "Communist Party USA." These are published as videos on the party's own site, and the boundary between a podcast and an official video programme can disappear entirely here.

### Monthly Review and MR Online

Monthly Review's own about page sets out its purpose at length. It says: "Monthly Review began publication in New York City in May 1949. The first issue featured the lead article 'Why Socialism?' by Albert Einstein. From the beginning, Monthly Review spoke for a critical but spirited socialism, independent of any political organization… published pioneering analyses of political economy, imperialism, and Third World struggles, drawing on the rich legacy of Marxist thought without being bound to any narrow view or party line." It names six editors: Paul M. Sweezy and Leo Huberman originally, Harry Magdoff from 1968, Ellen Meiksins Wood from 1997, then John Bellamy Foster and Robert W. McChesney. It states that "In 2006, MR began a daily web magazine, MRzine, which in 2017 was migrated to a new project, MR Online, a forum for collaboration and communication between radical activists, writers, and scholars around the world." The same page records that "in 1952, Monthly Review Press published its first title, I. F. Stone's Hidden History of the Korean War."

### Jacobin and Catalyst

Jacobin's about page states: "Jacobin is a leading voice of the American left, offering socialist perspectives on politics, economics, and culture. The print magazine is released quarterly and reaches 75,000 subscribers, in addition to a web audience of over 3,000,000 a month." It lists Bhaskar Sunkara as Editorial Director and Micah Uetricht as Editor, and a "Jacobin A/V Producer", Conor Gillies. Catalyst's about page says: "Discussion of capitalism is not off the table any longer. Catalyst: A Journal of Theory and Strategy launches with the aim of doing everything it can to promote and deepen this conversation. Our focus is, as our title suggests, to develop a theory and strategy with capitalism as its target, both in the North and in the Global South." It names Vivek Chibber as Editor, Bashir Abu-Manneh as Review Editor and Bhaskar Sunkara as Publisher. It states that it is "published by Jacobin Foundation" and "sustained by the support of over 7,500 individual subscribers." The two sites share staff and a foundation but are separately catalogued because each has its own about page.

### New Left Review and Socialist Register

New Left Review's about page describes "A 160-page journal published every two months from London" that "analyses world politics, the global economy, state powers and protest movements; contemporary social theory, history and philosophy; cinema, literature, heterodox art and aesthetics". It carries "signed editorials on political issues of the day" and has been published since its 1960 launch. It lists an Editorial Committee of 27 named members and editor Susan Watkins. Socialist Register's own about page says it "was founded by Ralph Miliband and John Saville in 1964 as 'an annual survey of movements and ideas' from the standpoint of the independent new left". It is "currently edited by Greg Albo, Stephen Maher and Catarina Principe" and "is published annually by Merlin Press in the UK, Monthly Review Press in the USA and Fernwood Books in Canada". That is an example of cooperation between radical presses.

### Tempest, Links and Green Left

Tempest's about page states: "We share a commitment to revolutionary socialism from below, united by a vision of replacing capitalism with a society rooted in the interests of the majority," and "Tempest aims to contribute to the reconstitution of revolutionary politics and organization in the United States and Canada today." Its resources include "Audio", "Debates" and "Videos and panels." Links states: "Links International Journal of Socialist Renewal is a journal for a post-Cold War left. It is a journal that rejects the Stalinist distortion of the socialist project… is committed to taking steps to bring together the forces for socialism in the world today… Inspired by the Latin American revolutions, Links is also a journal for 'Socialism of the 21st century'… It is committed to a democratic, participatory socialism." It adds that it "seeks to promote the international exchange of information, experiences of struggle, theoretical analysis and views on strategies and tactics within the international left", and calls itself "the sister publication of Green Left."

### Red Flag and Against the Current

Red Flag's own homepage presents reported news such as "Victorian election an opportunity to rebuild socialist organisation", theory such as "Why Marx's theory of surplus-value and exploitation matters to workers today", and a "Socialism 101" series with "The case for socialist organisation", "Why we carry the red flag" and "Lenin in his own words: five key texts." It states: "Help us build the socialist movement," and "New editions of Red Flag are released fortnightly. Subscribe to a print or digital subscription." Against the Current's about page says it "is the analytical and activist journal sponsored by Solidarity", that "debates are frequent and informative, with the goal of promoting discussion among activists, organizers, and scholars on the Left", and that this goes back "to the inception of the original series of Against the Current way back in 1980." It closes: "The struggle for a socialist, sustainable society is the future for survival, and we need each other!" and prints its editorial board and advisory editors by name.

### International Socialist Review, Socialist Worker and Political Affairs

The ISR's about page is explicit about both politics and status: "The ISR is dedicated to advancing socialist theory and practice in the U.S. and internationally. We stand in the International Socialist tradition, affirming our commitment to 'socialism from below,' the self-emancipation of workers and the oppressed, the struggle against imperialism and for national liberation, and the building of a socialist current rooted in all of those struggles." A notice dated 1 September 2021 adds: "After twenty-two years, the International Socialist Review… suspended publication in 2019. CERSC is committed to maintaining the archive of ISR articles at ISReview.org." It names Paul D'Amato as editor and Joel Geier, Lance Selfa, Ahmed Shawki and Sherry Wolf as associate editors. SocialistWorker.org's about page gives a parallel archive statement and states that the site "became a significant voice of independent media in the U.S."

The magazine describes Political Affairs on CPUSA's site at [cpusa.org/authors/political-affairs](https://www.cpusa.org/authors/political-affairs/): "Political Affairs is an online magazine of ideas, politics and culture. Our mission is to go beyond simply giving an account of events to providing analysis and investigating what is new and changing in our world, from a working-class point of view. We start from the most basic fact of life: the ongoing struggle between the working class and the capitalist class… While we are partisan, Marxism is not the private property of any person or group. We print a wide variety of views in our quest for truth. Discussion and debate are the only ways to develop better and more useful ideas… Political Affairs is affiliated with the Communist Party, USA." The description sets out the party-magazine function.

### Pathfinder Press

Pathfinder Press publishes a catalogue and a publicity apparatus. Its subject index includes "Writings of Marx and Engels", "Writings of Leon Trotsky", "Lenin and the Communist International", "Materialist World View", "Revolutionary Politics of the Working Class", "Education for Socialists Bulletins", "Cuban Revolution in the World", "Black Liberation", "Women's Emancipation Struggle", "Trade Unions: Past, Present, and Future" and "Africa". Its author index includes Marx, Engels, Lenin, Trotsky, Fidel Castro, Che Guevara, Malcolm X, W.E.B. Du Bois, Thomas Sankara, Nelson Mandela and Rosa Luxemburg. Its [promotional material page](https://www.pathfinderpress.com/pages/promotional-leaflets-and-reviews) offers free flyers titled "Cuba's Socialist Revolution", "Black Liberation", "Women's Emancipation", "Trade Unions and Working Class Struggle", "Three Books by Thomas Sankara" and "NEW INTERNATIONAL MAGAZINE". It states that these are "Perfect for your bookstore, library or classroom display, or just to let your friends know about a book you'd like them to read." The site's own blog reports sales at library conferences and book fairs, including the 2026 Joint Conference of Librarians of Color and a Jordan book fair featuring Arabic titles. That describes how the press sells and gives away its books.

### International Publishers

The publisher's own about page gives the fullest institutional history in this section. It states: "International Publishers was founded by Alexander L. Trachtenberg (1885-1966) in 1924. Trachtenberg, a personal acquaintance of V.I. Lenin, leader of the Bolshevik Revolution, was a Marxist writer, educator, and publisher, as well as a founding member of the Communist Party, USA. Under his stewardship International Publishers became a leading publisher of Marxist, Communist, labor, African American, and other literature." It continues: "Like other Communists, during the 1950s McCarthy era Red Scare Trachtenberg was hounded, harassed, called before the House Un-American Activities Committee, and eventually jailed for advocating Marxism-Leninism." It names James S. Allen, CPUSA organiser and editor of the *Southern Worker*, Betty Smith and Tony Pecinovsky as successive heads. It states that "Throughout its 100-year history International Publishers has maintained a commitment to publishing high quality Marxist literature, fiction and nonfiction." The page quotes Trachtenberg's 1936 note at the foot of the book's title page: "The figure is symbolic of the masses of people the publishers try to reach with the most significant economic and social literature of the time. The people, armed with the idea (the book), can look confidently toward the future." Its catalogue includes the *Communist Manifesto*, Lenin's *State and Revolution*, Herbert Aptheker's *American Negro Slave Revolts*, Philip S. Foner's 11-volume *History of the Labor Movement in the United States*, and the 50-volume Marx-Engels Collected Works, a joint project with Lawrence & Wishart and Progress Publishers as stated on the same page.

### Foreign Languages Press, Monthly Review Press, Verso and Haymarket

FLP's English "Introduction" states: "Founded in 1952, Foreign Languages Press (FLP) is the largest international-facing publisher in the People's Republic of China. It is now part of China International Communications Group (CICG). Since its founding, FLP has served the cause of international communication for the Communist Party of China (CPC) and the Chinese government. It translates into multiple languages, and publishes works of China's national leaders, important documents of the Party and the state, government white papers, and a wide range of books that present China's real conditions, development philosophy and achievements." It describes the five volumes of *Xi Jinping: The Governance of China* as "the most authoritative works to comprehensively expound Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era", published in 44 languages and distributed in more than 180 countries. FLP's own site also operates a [Videos section](https://flp.com.cn/en/video/).

Monthly Review Press's purpose is stated on the magazine's about page, which records the 1952 first title and says "A generation of activists received no small part of their education as subscribers to the magazine and readers of Monthly Review Press books." Verso's about page states: "New Left Books was launched by New Left Review in 1970, and took as its logo the Tatlin Tower, a planned monument to the Third International"; "Verso, the left-hand page, was launched as a paperback imprint at the end of the seventies"; "A new edition of the Communist Manifesto, on the 150th anniversary of its original publication, quickly became a global bestseller". Verso is "proud to undertake the publication of the complete works of Rosa Luxemburg… in collaboration with the Rosa-Luxemburg-Stiftung and Karl Dietz Verlag." It concludes: "With a strong list and radical commitment, all at Verso Books hope that the launch of this new website will strengthen our links with our readers and authors."

Haymarket's about page states: "Haymarket Books is a radical, independent, nonprofit book publisher based in Chicago… Our mission is to publish books that contribute to struggles for social and economic justice. We strive to make our books a vibrant and organic part of social movements and the education and development of a critical, engaged, and internationalist Left." It names the Haymarket Martyrs, "whose 1886 struggle for the eight-hour day, which gave us May Day, the international workers' holiday, reminds workers around the world that ordinary people can organize and struggle for their own liberation". It states that "[s]ince our founding in 2001, Haymarket has published more than nine hundred titles", and that it is "the trade publishers of the acclaimed Historical Materialism Book Series" and manages "the popular Haymarket Books Live event series and podcast, and the annual Socialism Conference."

### Wellred Books and New Outlook Publishers

Wellred's own site uses the Lenin epigraph "Without revolutionary theory there can be no revolutionary movement". Its catalogue is organised under headings such as "Marxist Philosophy", "Historical Materialism", "Economics", "Russian Revolution", "Political Theory", "Revolutionary Strategy", "Marxist classics" and "Featured Classics", including Lenin's *Imperialism*, *State and Revolution*, *'Left-Wing' Communism*, Engels' *Anti-Dühring* and Trotsky's *History of the Russian Revolution*. Its identity as "the publishing house of the Revolutionary Communist International" is stated on the RCI's own [podcast page](https://podcast.marxist.com/). New Outlook Publishers' about page states: "When we started in 2016, our passion for the working people drove us to create a publishing house to preserve and continue our historic movement, build class-consciousnesses, and grow our movement within the United States." Its catalogue categories include "Marxist-Leninist Theory", "Communist Party Structure", "Jones Foster School for Party Education", "The Communist", "New Masses", "Political Economy" and "Soviet Union / United States / China", with titles such as *Readings in Leninism Volume 1: What is Leninism?* and *PCUSA and How It Works*.

## Zines, small-scale distribution, newsletters and documentary collectives

Small projects often do not publish an about page. A distro is a small-scale distribution network that sells or gives away pamphlets, flyers and books outside commercial bookshops. The projects below state their own existence and politics.

The [PSMLS Media page](https://peoplesschool.us/media/) states: "PSMLS Flyers… Click to download the full image. The first 2 flyers can be printed and hung up on bulletin boards, with tape etc. The 3rd flyer with the checkered background has been made for thermal printers, which print only print in grayscale, but which can be posted up as stickers. Lastly, we have our brochure available for download as well. Help us spread our propaganda!" It offers a "Brochure", "Posters" and a "Business Card", a flyer operation attached to an educational project. Pathfinder Press offers free downloadable flyers for bookstores, libraries and classrooms, as described above. Red Flag offers a newsletter and states that "New editions of Red Flag are released fortnightly. Subscribe to a print or digital subscription." Its footer states: "Original Red Flag content is subject to a Creative Commons licence and may be republished under the terms listed here," a licensing stance that allows others to copy and redistribute the material. Green Left offers "up to two newsletters a week" plus "a local event newsletter", alongside its print newspaper and its video and podcast feeds. Tempest states: "Join our email list" and "We don't receive money from any outside sources because we believe that the Left needs financial independence as well as political independence. So we rely on the generosity of our readers and supporters." Monthly Review invites readers to "Keep up to date with all the latest socialist news from MR."

The SocialistWorker.org about page describes "archives of paper editions dating back to 1977 and its website dating back to 2001", a historical documentation project. Proletarian TV, the RCP and CPB video operations, Green Left's video team, FLP's Videos section and People's Daily Online's Video channel are described above. The chapter does not claim that any of them is a legally separate collective; their own sites present them as in-house audiovisual operations.

## Official party and state media

A few outlets are official publications of movements or states rather than independent projects. Their self-description is stated on their own sites. [Granma](https://www.granma.cu/) has the site title "Granma, Órgano oficial del PCC" and the meta description "Órgano Oficial del Comité Central del Partido Comunista de Cuba. Fundado el 3 de octubre de 1965. Actualización de noticias 24 horas", meaning "Official Organ of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of Cuba. Founded 3 October 1965." Its site links the "Diario Granma" YouTube channel. [People's Daily Online, English](https://en.people.cn/) is the official English portal of the People's Daily. Its footer links "About People's Daily Online" and its menu includes a dedicated "Video" channel; the site presents itself as an organ of the CPC's central press. [Foreign Languages Press](https://flp.com.cn/en/about/jigou/) says it has "served the cause of international communication for the Communist Party of China (CPC) and the Chinese government." [Political Affairs](https://www.cpusa.org/authors/political-affairs/) ends its own description with: "Political Affairs is affiliated with the Communist Party, USA." [Communist Party USA](https://www.cpusa.org/about-us/) states "Founded in 1919"; its "Party Voices" and "Videos" sections are official party communication channels.

## What movement media do

The outlets above are not just collections of posts and files. Their own pages describe four overlapping functions.

**Teaching theory.** The most explicit statement comes from the Communist Party of Britain: CommieCast "is our way of getting across the Communist Party's perspective on domestic and international news and developments as well as being a tool for Marxist education" ([CommieCast](https://communistparty.org.uk/commiecast/)). The PSMLS puts it in the language of party-school tradition: "part of a long line of American communist party sponsored schools… We were founded in 2016… We hold classes online every week… These are public classes on Zoom… The classes cost nothing and the knowledge is worth everything!" ([PSMLS](https://peoplesschool.us/)). The RCP separates "theory and education" from current affairs on its own pages. The RCI's School of Communism states that "Communists place a great deal of importance on education and theoretical study" and describes its conference as "a school of communism." The CPGB-ML's video site carries a "learn" menu with "marxist study programme" and "marxist videos", while Wellred Books uses Lenin's "Without revolutionary theory there can be no revolutionary movement" as its tagline. Haymarket states that it works for "the education and development of a critical, engaged, and internationalist Left". Monthly Review recalls that "A generation of activists received no small part of their education as subscribers to the magazine and readers of Monthly Review Press books." Red Flag Radio "aim[s] to explain socialist ideas in a clear and accessible way."

**Preserving historical documents.** The Marxists Internet Archive's [Charter](https://www.marxists.org/admin/legal/charter.htm) states its "mission… to provide a free-to-the-world archive of the works of Marxists and other works relevant to the understanding of Marxism". It specifies that each language section "shall include a Writers Archive… containing the archives of authors who are dead or no longer politically active" and "may include a History Archive which focuses on primary sources" and "an Archive of Periodicals, which focuses on complete scans of long runs of journals." Its [introduction](https://www.marxists.org/admin/intro/index.htm) calls it "an all-volunteer, non-profit public library, established in its present form in 1998" and says it holds "the writings of close to 1000 authors" in "83 different languages". Its [history page](https://www.marxists.org/admin/intro/history/index.htm) narrates the project from a 1990 Usenet posting through the 1993 "Marx/Engels Virtual Library" to the July 1998 creation of marxists.org. It says: "We weren't doing this just to understand the world, but also to create a new level of consciousness in people the world over… to show the value of critique in this era of conformity."

Alongside the MIA, the ISR states "CERSC is committed to maintaining the archive of ISR articles at ISReview.org". SocialistWorker.org states that "archives of paper editions dating back to 1977 and its website dating back to 2001 are available". International Publishers recounts its role in the 50-volume Marx-Engels Collected Works, described on its own page as "a joint publishing project between International Publishers, Lawrence & Wishart, and Progress Publishers in Moscow" ([About, International Publishers](https://www.intpubnyc.com/about/)). FLP lists its publication of Party and state documents. Communist Party of Britain's CommieCast includes an episode titled "Not One Step Back from the Truth: Fighting Historical Revisionism". Red Flag publishes "Lenin in his own words: five key texts" and "Why history matters for socialists", the latter noting that applying socialist ideas "without consideration of the movement's historic practice… is among the worst… errors socialists can fall into."

**Organisational communication.** CommieCast is explicitly "our way of getting across the Communist Party's perspective." CPUSA's "Party Voices" sections, "Videos", "On The Issues" and "Local Activities", publish material attributed to "DC Metro CPUSA" and "Debbie Amis Bell Club, CPUSA", alongside national "GMR" videos. Political Affairs states that it is "affiliated with the Communist Party, USA", while remaining a forum in which "We print a wide variety of views in our quest for truth." The CPGB-ML's about page publishes its congress resolutions and "Party Programme and Rules". Its TV section carries statements from party representatives such as Joti Brar and Ranjeet Brar and from fraternal organisations including the Cuban ambassador and Venezuelan and DPRK representatives. The PSMLS Twitter embedding states "Sponsored by the PCUSA ☭." Granma's own masthead calls it the "Órgano oficial del Comité Central del Partido Comunista de Cuba." In each case the media carry an organisation's official position, report its activities and recruit. The RCP's podcast pages end with "Join the party"; the CPGB-ML's about page ends with "Join us! Donate and help support our vital work!"; Red Flag's homepage asks readers to "Help us build the socialist movement."

**International solidarity.** Political Affairs lists among its subjects "ending war and imperialism" and "international solidarity". FLP says it "facilitat[es] cultural communication, cooperation and mutual understanding between China and the rest of the world." Green Left states: "Green Left aims to amplify the voices of the progressive movements… We pledge to support the fight for justice for First Nations peoples." Its output includes "Video: How Palestine solidarity activists are being silenced" and interviews on "the climate policy we need." The CPGB-ML's own party statement lists congress resolutions including "Solidarity with Syria and the axis of resistance", "Solidarity with the Democratic People's Republic of Korea", "Hands off Ukraine", "Defend the People's Republic of China against US-led imperialist aggression", "Free the Miami Five" and "Building solidarity with the DPRK". Its Proletarian TV includes "Pawns or heroes? Cuba's proud tradition of international medical solidarity" and "Cuban ambassador: From Cuba to Gaza: the resistance stands firm!" Communist Party of Britain's CommieCast list includes "Venezuela: a Crisis of Capitalism? A view from the Communist Party of Venezuela" and "Ukraine and China: The Quiet American Wars." Links describes itself as a forum for "the international exchange of information, experiences of struggle, theoretical analysis and views on strategies and tactics within the international left" and publishes documents from "supporting parties." Tempest states that its members act "for internationalist solidarity with Palestine and other struggles." These statements show a shared function: movement media connect local organising to a claimed global movement.

The labels in this chapter, "Marxist-Leninist", "Trotskyist", "socialist from below", "ecosocialist", "democratic socialist" and "scientific socialist", are terms the outlets use of themselves. The chapter records what each outlet says it is. Where outlets attack each other in print, the chapter reports the competing claims without judging between them.
