## Biography

The existing chapter on communist movements in the Philippines and Japan ([Two Archipelagos](/philippines-japan)) covers the Hukbalahap, the 1950 State Department paper on the Huks, the Army counterinsurgency chronology, the re-establishment of the Communist Party of the Philippines (CPP) on 26 December 1968, the founding of the New People's Army (NPA) in March 1969, the break with the older pro-Moscow party and Sison's account of torture after his arrest on 10 November 1977; this profile sets out his argument as an argument, gives his own definitions, examines his economic writings, checks his book titles against catalogues and publisher records, and engages the criticism made against him.

The dates 8 February 1939 and 16 December 2022 are given by the INPS memorial archive's "About" page, but that page could not be fetched directly, so they are recorded here as first-party claims seen in a search excerpt rather than independently verified institutional facts. The NDFP's own notices confirm that Sison died in a hospital in Utrecht on 16 December 2022, aged 83 ([NDFP notice, **Primary Official**](https://ndfp.info/prof-jose-ma-sison-1939-2022/); [NDFP National Council, **Primary Official**](https://ndfp.info/statement-of-the-ndfp-national-council-on-the-passing-of-beloved-comrade-jose-maria-ka-joma-sison/)). The IIRE library author record gives the fuller form Jose Maria Canlas Sison, the variant "Amado Guerrero" and the birth year 1939 ([IIRE, **Primary Official**](https://library.iire.org/index.php/node/370)). Abinales, writing as a scholar, describes him as born into a prominent Spanish-speaking family of Chinese descent in Cabugao, Ilocos Sur; the OCR of the accessible page garbles the place as "Cayuga, Locos Sure" ([Abinales, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://docslib.org/doc/689155/jose-maria-sison-and-the-philippine-revolution-a-critique-of-an-interface1)).

Sison's own account supplies the intellectual chronology. He says his study of Philippine history and of the old Communist Party of the Philippine Islands began in the latter half of the 1950s, and that in 1966 he and his comrades separated from the Lava leadership of that party ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)). The political line he was working out in those years was what he called national democracy: a stage of revolution in which a working-class-led alliance of workers, peasants, the petty bourgeoisie and the national bourgeoisie sought national independence and democratic reforms before a separate socialist stage. The term named a political line, not simply a commitment to elections or civil liberties ([*Struggle for National Democracy*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.marxists.org/archive/sison/sison-struggle-for-national-democracy.pdf)). In the preface to the third edition of that book he records that it gathers speeches and essays from 1964–68, written while he was founding chairman of Kabataang Makabayan from 30 November 1964, vice-chairman or general secretary of the Socialist Party of the Philippines, and general secretary of the Movement for the Advancement of Nationalism, and that he went underground in 1968 ([*Struggle for National Democracy*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.marxists.org/archive/sison/sison-struggle-for-national-democracy.pdf)). He was an English major at the University of the Philippines, published poems in the *Philippine Collegian* and brought out a first book of poems in 1961, at twenty-two; he had attended Ateneo and Letran in high school ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)). The CPP was re-established on 26 December 1968 and the NPA founded on 29 March 1969, dates given in his interview and in the party documents he drafted ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf); [*Foundation for Resuming the Philippine Revolution*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/Sison/SelectedWritingsOfJoseMariaSison-1968-1991-FoundationForResumingThePhilippineRevolution-1968-1972.pdf)).

His preface to *Detention and Defiance against Dictatorship* fixes the prison chronology: "Detention and Defiance against Dictatorship covers the period from my capture on November 10, 1977 to the fall of the Marcos fascist dictatorship on February 25, 1986." He says he was presented to Marcos on the day of capture, that the worst torture was solitary confinement lasting more than five years, interrupted by partial isolation with his wife Julie in 1980–81 when their fourth child was born, and that the confinement ended in 1985 after a Supreme Court ruling ([*Detention and Defiance*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/Sison/SelectedWritingsOfJoseMariaSison-1968-1991-DetentionAndDefianceAgainstDictatorship-1977-1986.pdf)). This confirms the arrest date already used in the existing chapter. The editorial introductions to the kites interview and its NDFP republication say he was captured "in 1974"; that date conflicts with his own preface and with the chapter, and is treated here as an editorial error rather than a verified fact ([kites editorial page, editorial note, not evidence](https://kites-journal.org/2022/10/27/on-foreign-monsters-and-the-peoples-war-that-persists)). He says he was abroad from the later part of 1986, had his Philippine passport cancelled in 1988, sought asylum in the Netherlands, obtained recognition as a political refugee from the Dutch Council of State in 1992, and lived in Utrecht with Julieta de Lima ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)). These are his own claims in a friendly interview, not findings of a court or government record opened here.

## Intellectual tradition

Sison wrote inside the Marxist-Leninist-Maoist current and called the CPP a party "of Mao Tse-tung's thought"; the party's 1968 programme and constitution are printed in *Foundation for Resuming the Philippine Revolution* ([**Primary Theoretical**](https://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/Sison/SelectedWritingsOfJoseMariaSison-1968-1991-FoundationForResumingThePhilippineRevolution-1968-1972.pdf)). He distinguished his line from the old party led by the Lavas, which he called revisionist, from Soviet "social-imperialism" and, later, from "Dengist counter-revolution" in China. His anti-revisionism did not stop tactical flexibility: in 2022 he said the CPP would not publicly act as "the ideological arbiter" of which countries were truly socialist, and that "there's no use trying to apply the ideological purity" of Marxism-Leninism-Maoism to relations with non-communist entities ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)). Scalice reads the same commitment differently: he argues that the CPP and the old PKP shared a Stalinist two-stage programme and differed over leadership and international alignment, not over that basic programme ([Scalice, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://www.josephscalice.com/2020/07/fifty-years-since-the-publication-of-philippine-society-and-revolution-introduction/)). That is a scholar's interpretation of the texts, not Sison's self-description.

The label "national democracy" was Sison's own, and he used it to name a political line rather than a party sect. In *Struggle for National Democracy* he wrote: "There is only one nationalism that we appreciate. It is that which refers to the national democratic revolution, the Philippine revolution, whose main tasks now are the liquidation of imperialism and feudalism to achieve full national freedom and democratic reforms" ([**Primary Theoretical**](https://www.marxists.org/archive/sison/sison-struggle-for-national-democracy.pdf)). He placed on one side "the compradors and the big landlords" and on the other "the broad masses of our people, composed of the working class and the peasantry ... the petty bourgeoisie ... and the national bourgeoisie" ([**Primary Theoretical**](https://www.marxists.org/archive/sison/sison-struggle-for-national-democracy.pdf)). The label therefore described a class alliance and a stage of revolution, not a general theory of democracy. Within the Philippine left the line was contested. Lagman, writing from the CPP's Metro Manila–Rizal Regional Committee in 1994, accused Sison of substituting the Maoist "mass line" for the Marxist-Leninist "class line" and of producing "a 'people's program' for a national democratic revolution, not a class program of the revolutionary proletariat" ([Lagman, PPDR, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lagman/works/ppdr.htm)). The CPP history archive also records later splits between "Reaffirmists" and "Rejectionists" and internal journals such as *Bulatlatan*, but those records are used here only as a finding aid ([finding aid, not evidence](https://www.marxists.org/history/philippines/index.htm)).

## Major contributions

The first was the two-stage theory of the Philippine revolution, stated most compactly in chapter three of *Philippine Society and Revolution*: "Under the present concrete conditions of Philippine society which are semicolonial and semifeudal, the Communist Party has to wage a national democratic revolution of a new type, a people's democratic revolution. Though its leadership is proletarian, the Philippine Revolution is not yet a proletarian-socialist revolution. We should not confuse the national-democratic stage and the socialist stage of the Philippine Revolution. Only after the national-democratic stage has been completed can the proletarian revolutionary leadership carry out the socialist revolution as the transitional stage towards communism" ([**Primary Theoretical**](http://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/1970s/PhilippineSocietyAndRevolution-4ed.pdf)). The "new type" lay in working-class leadership: "The effective class leadership in the Philippine Revolution is now in the hands of the proletariat and no longer in the hands of the bourgeoisie or any of its strata as was previously the case in the old type of national-democratic revolution" ([**Primary Theoretical**](http://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/1970s/PhilippineSocietyAndRevolution-4ed.pdf)). In the 2020 online question and answer he put the aim in a sentence: "The most important aim of the national democratic revolution is to achieve full national independence and people's democracy" ([NDFP transcript, **Primary Theoretical**](https://ndfp.info/on-the-peoples-democratic-revolution)).

The second contribution was his characterization of Philippine society as "semicolonial and semifeudal", with imperialism, feudalism and bureaucrat capitalism named as "the three historical evils" ([*Philippine Society and Revolution*, **Primary Theoretical**](http://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/1970s/PhilippineSocietyAndRevolution-4ed.pdf)). Here "semi-feudalism", in his own spelling "semifeudalism", meant an economy in which land remained the principal means of production and a comprador bourgeoisie produced raw materials for export, without a national industrial bourgeoisie; it was his claim about how the economy worked, not an established finding (his fullest definition came in 1983, discussed below). "Semicolonial" was the companion claim about political subjection to a foreign power. The third contribution was the strategic line of protracted people's war: armed struggle from rural base areas, with the peasantry as the main force, the working class as leader, and the united front as the third "weapon" alongside the party and the army. In the 2022 interview he described the NPA as growing "from small and weak to big and strong in an archipelago" and said *Specific Characteristics of Our People's War* answered comrades who doubted that an island country could sustain a people's war ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)). A fourth was the practice of "rectification": the 1968 founding document "Rectify Errors, Rebuild the Party!" and later rectification campaigns, which he defended in 2022 as the means by which the CPP had preserved its line through splits ([*Foundation for Resuming*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/Sison/SelectedWritingsOfJoseMariaSison-1968-1991-FoundationForResumingThePhilippineRevolution-1968-1972.pdf); [kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)).

Sison's own summary of what mattered is recorded in a 2010 *Ang Bayan* interview: "The key contributions of PSR are its characterization of Philippine society as semicolonial and semifeudal and the corresponding line of national and democratic revolution under the leadership of the working class" ([*Develop the People's Power*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/Sison/SelectedWritingsOfJoseMariaSison-2009-2015-V2-DevelopThePeople'sPower.pdf)). Scalice, while rejecting the politics, agrees on the scale of the effect: "The language of PSR regarding the goal of national democracy and the political tasks which this goal entailed spread widely, in certain periods becoming nearly ubiquitous" ([Scalice, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://www.josephscalice.com/2020/07/fifty-years-since-the-publication-of-philippine-society-and-revolution-introduction/)).

## Analysis of capitalism

Sison's account of capitalism was an account of monopoly capitalism operating through ownership of the means of production in a poor, agricultural country. In *Philippine Society and Revolution* he defined imperialism as monopoly capitalism and argued that the Philippines was kept as a supplier of raw materials and a market for finished goods. The mechanism was unequal exchange: "It is in the nature of modern imperialism to make possible only uneven and spasmodic development. The U.S. monopoly capitalists are merely interested in making superprofits from the colonial exchange of raw materials from the Philippines and fully processed commodities from the United States, from direct investments that rake in a higher rate of profit from colonies and semicolonies and from the practice of international usury" ([**Primary Theoretical**](http://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/1970s/PhilippineSocietyAndRevolution-4ed.pdf)).

His most specific definition of "semifeudalism" came in answers written in prison in 1983 and published as "Jose Ma. Sison on the Mode of Production in the Philippines" by Julieta L. Sison, his wife, from their discussions and her notes of his answers ([*Critique of Philippine Economy and Politics*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://philippinerevolution.nu/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/03-Critique-of-PH-Econ-and-Pol.pdf)). There he wrote: "Semifeudalism can be used in two senses: (1) to sum up the economy that is shackled by two moribund forces — imperialism and feudalism; and (2) to refer to the dominance of the comprador big bourgeoisie and the kind of production it promotes (primarily raw-material production for export)." He explained why he rejected the alternatives: "We cannot call the dominant production relations capitalist because it is a comprador big bourgeoisie rather than a national industrial bourgeoisie that has hegemony over them"; terms such as "semicapitalist" or "in transition to capitalism" would "obscure the persistence of feudalism and the commanding position of semifeudalism"; and "The Philippines is not at all on the way to becoming fully capitalist" ([**Primary Theoretical**](https://philippinerevolution.nu/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/03-Critique-of-PH-Econ-and-Pol.pdf)). The same essay listed the productive forces: no basic or machine-tool industries, imported equipment paid for by raw-material exports and loans, land still the principal means of production, tractors on no more than four per cent of agricultural land, and a large reserve army of cheap labour ([**Primary Theoretical**](https://philippinerevolution.nu/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/03-Critique-of-PH-Econ-and-Pol.pdf)). These are his figures, taken from official and other sources as he cited them; they were not independently checked here.

In the kites interview he restated the claim in terms of import dependence: "The Philippines lacks an industrial foundation and cannot be considered industrial capitalist"; what passed for electronics and transport manufacturing was "merely assembly of finished parts and components from abroad" ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)). The class attached to this system was the comprador big bourgeoisie, which he called "the chief financial and trading agent of foreign monopoly capitalism" and, in its landholding role, "the big comprador-landlord class" ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)). Bureaucrat capitalists were the state agents of these interests: "Bureaucrat capitalism is the social basis of fascism" ([*Philippine Society and Revolution*, **Primary Theoretical**](http://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/1970s/PhilippineSocietyAndRevolution-4ed.pdf)). Landlordism mattered because it supplied the social base of imperialist rule: "It is in this sense that domestic feudalism is the social base of U.S. imperialism. The persistence of landlord exploitation is in turn under the counterrevolutionary protection of U.S. imperialism" ([**Primary Theoretical**](http://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/1970s/PhilippineSocietyAndRevolution-4ed.pdf)). The remedy he drew from this was agrarian revolution, which would "destroy the links between U.S. imperialism and feudalism and deprive the former of its social base" ([**Primary Theoretical**](http://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/1970s/PhilippineSocietyAndRevolution-4ed.pdf)).

## Analysis of socialism

Sison did not write a detailed blueprint for a completed socialist society. He wrote a programme for a transitional people's democracy and a short account of what would follow it. The 1968 "Program for a People's Democratic Revolution" began: "The basic condition of the Philippines today is that of a semicolonial and semifeudal country dominated by the US imperialists, the comprador big bourgeoisie, the landlords and the bureaucrat capitalists" ([*Foundation for Resuming*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/Sison/SelectedWritingsOfJoseMariaSison-1968-1991-FoundationForResumingThePhilippineRevolution-1968-1972.pdf)). The 1972 "Guide for Establishing the People's Democratic Government" is the most concrete institutional text. It provides that the government "is led by the proletariat and is based on the toiling masses of the proletariat and the peasantry" while having "a united front character, with all democratic classes, including the petty bourgeoisie, the national bourgeoisie and others, supporting it and participating in it"; that "the system of the People's Democratic Government shall be based on the principle of democratic centralism"; and that the basic unit is the barrio people's government, whose revolutionary committee handles land reform, the militia, the people's court, taxes and local defence ([*On the People's Democratic Revolution*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://philippinerevolution.nu/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/04-On-the-PDR.pdf)). A National People's Congress, elected through provincial governments and representatives recommended by the NDFP, would be the highest organ ([**Primary Theoretical**](https://philippinerevolution.nu/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/04-On-the-PDR.pdf)). These are rules in a programme, not descriptions of institutions that existed nationwide.

On the transition, he told the ND Online School in 2020: "The national democratic revolution is the preparation for the socialist revolution." He said that on seizure of political power the socialist revolution "can commence immediately with the working class and its vanguard party building immediately the political system to unite and govern the people and taking over all the commanding heights of the economy, all strategic industries, main transport lines and all sources of energy and raw materials. The state proceeds to build socialist industry and cooperativize and mechanize agriculture in a series of five-year plans" ([NDFP transcript, **Primary Theoretical**](https://ndfp.info/on-the-peoples-democratic-revolution)). He also allowed for "transitional measures of a bourgeois democratic character, like completing the land reform program and integrating the patriotic bourgeoisie into joint state and private enterprises" ([**Primary Theoretical**](https://ndfp.info/on-the-peoples-democratic-revolution)). On armed force he wrote: "History has shown that the proletariat has never won a new democratic revolution and establish socialism without armed revolution. There has never been a case of the bourgeoisie giving up its state power and its private ownership of the means of production voluntarily and peacefully" ([**Primary Theoretical**](https://ndfp.info/on-the-peoples-democratic-revolution)). When asked in 2022 whether Cuba, Vietnam, North Korea or Venezuela were socialist, he declined to judge, saying the CPP would not "pose publicly as the ideological arbiter" and would work with states that declared themselves anti-imperialist ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)).

Lagman's criticism bears directly on this section. He argued that the 1968 programme was "a Party Program without the struggle for socialism and without a separate section on workers' demands in the period of the democratic revolution", and that Sison treated socialism as a distant "second step" rather than "a socialist starting point, a socialist framework, a socialist viewpoint" ([Lagman, PPDR, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lagman/works/ppdr.htm)). The text of the programme and the 2020 answers should be read alongside that objection: the socialist perspective is asserted in both, but the detailed institutional writing is mostly about the preceding democratic stage.

The dossier also records that the first publication date of *Philippine Society and Revolution* is inconsistent across sources: Sison says a mimeographed first edition in October 1969; Scalice documents serialization from July 1970 and a compiled volume by the end of 1970; *Ang Bayan* in 2010 marked forty years; Pulang Tala's edition is generally dated 1971. All are reported here; none is silently preferred.

## Recommended reading

The dossier's own shortlist follows, with no titles or links added.

- *Philippine Society and Revolution* (1970/71) — The single text in which the semi-colonial and semi-feudal analysis, the class alliances and the two-stage programme are set out together.
- *Struggle for National Democracy* (1967) — The earlier essays and speeches where "national democracy" is first worked out in legal, persuasive language before it became a party programme.
- *Foundation for Resuming the Philippine Revolution* (2013) — Collects the 1968 rectification report, programme and constitution, so the founding break can be read in Sison's own drafts.
- *Critique of Philippine Economy and Politics* (2021), especially the 1983 "Mode of Production" question and answer — The clearest statement of what he meant by "semifeudal" and why he rejected "capitalist" or "transition to capitalism".
- *Detention and Defiance against Dictatorship* (2013) — The prison writings, including the 1979 torture statement and the account of how he smuggled articles and poems out, show the theoretical work continuing under detention.

## Intellectual influences

Sison's own writing points first to Mao. *Philippine Society and Revolution* takes its epigraphs from Mao and states that the CPP is guided by "Marxism-Leninism-Mao Tsetung Thought"; the 2010 interview calls the application of that theory to Philippine history "the biggest theoretical challenge" ([**Primary Theoretical**](http://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/1970s/PhilippineSocietyAndRevolution-4ed.pdf); [*Develop the People's Power*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/Sison/SelectedWritingsOfJoseMariaSison-2009-2015-V2-DevelopThePeople'sPower.pdf)). Scalice and Guillermo independently argue that the structure of *Philippine Society and Revolution* follows a Maoist model, and that D. N. Aidit's *Indonesian Society and the Indonesian Revolution* (1957) supplied a closer template: both are primers that move from society to revolution, distinguish a comprador from a national bourgeoisie and conclude that the task is national and democratic rather than immediately socialist ([Scalice, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://www.josephscalice.com/2020/07/fifty-years-since-the-publication-of-philippine-society-and-revolution-introduction/); [Guillermo, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://englishkyoto-seas.org/wp-content/uploads/SEAS0701_Guillermo.pdf)). Guillermo records Sison's own answer to the question of influence, in an email of 6 December 2015: the PKI Politburo's self-criticism after the 1965 massacre "had the greatest influence on the CPP, more than any work by Aidit" ([Guillermo, quoting Sison, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://englishkyoto-seas.org/wp-content/uploads/SEAS0701_Guillermo.pdf)).

Guillermo also documents a 1962 study fellowship in Indonesia, Sison's translations of Chairil Anwar's poetry, his friendship with the PKI-linked guerrilla Bakri Ilyas, and his editorship of the *Progressive Review*, which carried material on Sukarno-era Indonesia ([Guillermo, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://englishkyoto-seas.org/wp-content/uploads/SEAS0701_Guillermo.pdf)). Guillermo quotes Sison from the 1989 interview book: "Inevitably, the revolutionary movements of pre-1965 Indonesia, Vietnam and China had a strong impact on Filipino revolutionaries because these stood for armed revolution to achieve national liberation and democracy; and were in the neighbourhood of the Philippines" ([Guillermo quoting Sison, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://englishkyoto-seas.org/wp-content/uploads/SEAS0701_Guillermo.pdf)). In 2022 Sison placed himself alongside Abimael Guzmán, Charu Majumdar and Ibrahim Kaypakkaya as part of a generation that took up Mao's call to build new communist parties ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)). He named Marx, Engels, Lenin and Mao as the sources of the theory he taught in prison, and later wrote critiques of the Frankfurt School, the Austrian School, French postmodernism and British analytic philosophy ([*Detention and Defiance*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/Sison/SelectedWritingsOfJoseMariaSison-1968-1991-DetentionAndDefianceAgainstDictatorship-1977-1986.pdf); [kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)). Teodoro Agoncillo, professor and chair of history at the University of the Philippines, wrote the introduction to the first edition of *Struggle for National Democracy* in April 1967 ([**Primary Theoretical**](https://www.marxists.org/archive/sison/sison-struggle-for-national-democracy.pdf)).

## Influence and legacy

The strongest evidence of influence is internal to the movement. *Ang Bayan* said in 2010 that for forty years *Philippine Society and Revolution* had served "as the CPP's principal reference and guide in laying down the basic principles of the two-stage revolution in the Philippines based on the analysis of concrete conditions of the semicolonial and semifeudal system" ([*Develop the People's Power*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/Sison/SelectedWritingsOfJoseMariaSison-2009-2015-V2-DevelopThePeople'sPower.pdf)). Scalice, an independent historian, writes that the book "shaped not only the program and perspective of the party itself, but also the language and orientation of emerging mass movements over the course of five decades" and moulded Philippine humanities and social sciences, though he calls that influence in part "subtle, diffusive" ([Scalice, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://www.josephscalice.com/2020/07/fifty-years-since-the-publication-of-philippine-society-and-revolution-introduction/)). Abinales records that during the First Quarter Storm students declared their fealty to Amado Guerrero and that a building at the University of the Philippines was renamed after the CPP chairman ([Abinales, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://docslib.org/doc/689155/jose-maria-sison-and-the-philippine-revolution-a-critique-of-an-interface1)). The NDFP's death statement credits him with leading the establishment of the front on 24 April 1973 and inspiring the sectoral mass organisations that became its members ([NDFP, **Primary Official**](https://ndfp.info/statement-of-the-ndfp-national-council-on-the-passing-of-beloved-comrade-jose-maria-ka-joma-sison/)). Internationally, Sison chaired the International Preparatory Committee that established the International League of Peoples' Struggle from 1998 to 2001 and was its chair from 2004 to 2019, by his own account ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)). Claims about his continuing directional authority after 1986 are contested inside the movement itself, as the *Bulatlatan* debates catalogued by the Marxists Internet Archive indicate; those documents were not opened here ([finding aid, not evidence](https://www.marxists.org/history/philippines/index.htm)).

## Criticisms and controversies

The most detailed scholarly critique of the autobiographical source is Abinales's essay on the book he calls *Interface*. Abinales reads it as a work written and rewritten after the 1986 rupture in the CPP, when the party was "beset with dissension, resignations, defections, and unprecedented internal debates"; he argues that Sison used it to "restore order within the movement by fusing his political views with accounts of his life to serve both as an example of revolutionary commitment and an inspiration for cadres". His conclusion is that "Interface, therefore, becomes both an autobiography and a political text where the personal merges and becomes subsumed into the political." He also notes that Sison's pre-eminence "appears to have been challenged as the CPP grew away from its Maoist origins towards attempting to create a Filipino Marxism" ([Abinales, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://docslib.org/doc/689155/jose-maria-sison-and-the-philippine-revolution-a-critique-of-an-interface1)). Only the opening part of this essay could be read in the HTML view, and its download link did not yield a PDF, so its later specific objections are not summarised here.

Scalice's objection is theoretical and historiographical. He calls *Philippine Society and Revolution* "to its core, a nationalist document", because Sison inscribed it to "all patriots" and not to the international working class; he notes that it says nothing about 1789, 1848, 1917 or the Chinese Revolution of 1926–27, does not mention Marx, Engels, Luxemburg or Trotsky except as a phrase-header, and is silent on the Indonesian PKI massacre, Vietnam and the crisis in the Chinese party ([Scalice, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://www.josephscalice.com/2020/07/fifty-years-since-the-publication-of-philippine-society-and-revolution-introduction/)). He reads the two-stage theory as a Stalinist inheritance in which a "progressive" section of the national bourgeoisie was to be an ally, and says the book had to show that the old party's failures came from the Lava leadership rather than from the programme ([Scalice, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://www.josephscalice.com/2020/07/fifty-years-since-the-publication-of-philippine-society-and-revolution-introduction/)). He rejects the plagiarism accusation as "absurd" political mud-slinging while still arguing that Sison followed a well-worn Maoist and Indonesian template ([Scalice, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://www.josephscalice.com/2020/07/fifty-years-since-the-publication-of-philippine-society-and-revolution-introduction/)). Guillermo reaches a similar qualified conclusion: Alex Magno's 2007 claim that Sison merely changed names and places in an Indonesian text was never proved, but the structural similarity to Aidit's primer is real, Sison relied on Otley Beyer's "Wave Migration Theory" for the peopling of the Philippines, a theory Guillermo says is no longer accepted, and the silence of the book on the 1965 Indonesian massacre is "all the more unsettling" given the PKI's early influence ([Guillermo, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://englishkyoto-seas.org/wp-content/uploads/SEAS0701_Guillermo.pdf)).

From inside the Philippine left, Lagman's 1994 essays are the sharpest theoretical challenge. In "PSR: The Semifeudal Alibi for Protracted War" he argues that "semifeudal and semicolonial" describes features of Philippine society but does not name its mode of production, and that "political expediency must not in any way become an excuse to obscure or evade the necessity for a theoretically precise definition ... Nor must this analysis become simply an alibi for a preconceived strategy of revolution." His section heading is "Semifeudalism: A Mode of Expression Not a Mode of Production". He reads Sison's own admission that natural economy had been eroded in favour of commodity economy as evidence that capitalism, not feudalism, was the basic process, and objects to the claim that feudalism was deliberately retained by imperialism as its "social base" ([Lagman, **Primary Theoretical**](https://libcom.org/library/psr-semifeudal-alibi-protracted-war)). In "PPDR: Class Line vs. Mass Line" he writes that the 1968 programme is "the best proof of his abandonment or ignorance of the most basic principles of Marxism-Leninism—the class struggle and scientific socialism", that it lacks a socialist maximum programme and a section on workers' demands, and that Sison reduced proletarian leadership to CPP leadership and substituted a peasant-led people's war for an independent workers' class line ([Lagman, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lagman/works/ppdr.htm)). These are disagreements within a shared Marxist vocabulary; they are not the same as the right-wing plagiarism allegations, which Guillermo found unsubstantiated.

A further controversy concerns the reliability of Sison's own retrospective account. His statements about torture, solitary confinement and smuggling were written for legal and political purposes under martial law and were published by his own movement; the existing chapter already states their limits. The 1984 article on the mode of production is unusual because it was written in answer to academic questioners and mediated by Julieta Sison, who states that she wrote freely from her notes of his answers on condition that she remain faithful to his ideas ([*Critique of Philippine Economy and Politics*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://philippinerevolution.nu/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/03-Critique-of-PH-Econ-and-Pol.pdf)). The legal cases in the Netherlands and the EU terrorist listing are reported in the kites interview as Sison's own account of victories in court; no court judgment was opened here ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)). The allegation that he coordinated the 1971 Plaza Miranda bombing was not verified against a court record or a book opened here and is not asserted as a finding.

## Related thinkers

- **Mao Zedong** — source of the semi-colonial/semi-feudal formula, the new-democracy framework, protracted people's war and rectification; Sison describes himself as part of the global generation that took up Mao's call to build new communist parties ([kites interview, **Primary Theoretical**](https://kites-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/on-foreign-monsters_-jms-interview-with-kites-sep-2022.pdf)).
- **D. N. Aidit** — the Indonesian PKI leader whose primer supplied a close model for *Philippine Society and Revolution*; Sison said the PKI's self-criticism after 1965 influenced the CPP more than any of Aidit's writings ([Guillermo, **Scholarly Secondary**](https://englishkyoto-seas.org/wp-content/uploads/SEAS0701_Guillermo.pdf)).
- **Vladimir Lenin** — cited throughout Sison's class analysis and the source, for Lagman, of the standards by which the 1968 programme was judged wanting ([*Philippine Society and Revolution*, **Primary Theoretical**](http://www.bannedthought.net/Philippines/CPP/1970s/PhilippineSocietyAndRevolution-4ed.pdf); [Lagman, **Primary Theoretical**](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lagman/works/ppdr.htm)).
- **Filemon "Popoy" Lagman** — former CPP cadre and leader of the Rejectionist current; his 1994 polemics dispute both the semi-feudal thesis and the strategic priority of rural armed struggle ([Lagman, **Primary Theoretical**](https://libcom.org/library/psr-semifeudal-alibi-protracted-war)).
- **Rainer Werning** — German solidarity activist and co-author of the interview book published in English as *The Philippine Revolution: The Leader's View* ([Archive catalogue, **Primary Official**](https://archive.org/details/philippinerevolu0000siso); [NDFP launch report, **Primary Official**](https://ndfp.info/ndfp-international-office-successfully-launches-english-and-german-editions-of-sison-book)).
- **Julieta de Lima Sison** — wife, editor of the selected-writings volumes, co-author of *Philippine Economy and Politics*, and the person who recorded and shaped the 1983 answers on the mode of production ([*Critique of Philippine Economy and Politics*, **Primary Theoretical**](https://philippinerevolution.nu/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/03-Critique-of-PH-Econ-and-Pol.pdf)).
- **Benedict Kerkvliet** — author of *The Huk Rebellion*, which supplies the peasant-side history that Sison's party documents treat as a lesson in past errors; discussed in the existing chapter and not repeated here.
