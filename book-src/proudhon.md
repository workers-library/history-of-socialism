In May 1846 Karl Marx, then in Brussels, wrote to a printer in Paris asking him to handle France in a regular correspondence between German, French and English socialists: "So far as France is concerned, we all of us believe that we could find no better correspondent than yourself." ([Karl Marx to Pierre-Joseph Proudhon, 5 May 1846, MECW translation](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1846/letters/46_05_05.htm)) Within eighteen months that correspondent had published a book-length public verdict against him. When the printer died in 1865, Marx wrote of him with respect. The quarrel can be followed in the letters and books themselves rather than in the labels later attached to it.

The printer was Pierre-Joseph Proudhon, born in 1809, the son of a cooper in Besançon and largely self-taught. His book *What is Property? An Inquiry into the Principle of Right and of Government* appeared in 1840, when he was thirty-one. He argued that neither labour nor occupation nor law could create property, and rejected majority sovereignty and the government of man by man. Mutualism, meaning a scheme in which workers organised their own credit and exchange, grew from that anti-political premise. He was also the first to call himself an anarchist, meaning an opponent of government rather than of order.

## A printer's challenge

The book's famous passage was a deliberate provocation. "Why, then, to this other question: What is property! may I not likewise answer, It is robbery, without the certainty of being misunderstood; the second proposition being no other than a transformation of the first?" ([Pierre-Joseph Proudhon, *What is Property?*, ch. I, 1840, translation by Benjamin R. Tucker](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/economics/proudhon/property/ch01.htm)) The phrase was not his: Brissot had used it before the French Revolution. His target was narrower than the slogan: property that let one person live from another's labour. He repeatedly disclaimed any wish to build a system.

In 1842 he stood before a jury in Besançon, charged with attacking property, inciting class hatred and insulting the government, and used the trial as a platform: "I have only written one thing in my life, gentlemen jurors, and I will tell you that thing right away, so there is no question: Property is robbery." ([Proudhon, court defence, Besançon assizes, 3 February 1842](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/economics/proudhon/1842/explanations-presented-to-the-public.html)) He set out a wages-and-deduction account of exploitation, using a shoemaker's five-franc pair, and told the jury that to abolish that robbery it was necessary to universalise it. They acquitted him after an hour. The trial report is an edited extract by someone else and the defence was self-serving, but the event made him a public figure.

Marx approached this man in 1846 with an offer that included paying postage. Proudhon's reply of 17 May set conditions that also diagnosed their difference: he would not lead a new orthodoxy and rejected political revolution as a means of reform. "I would therefore prefer to burn Property by a slow fire, rather than give it new strength by making a St Bartholomew's night of the proprietors ..." ([Proudhon to Karl Marx, 17 May 1846](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/economics/proudhon/letters/46_05_17.htm)), he wrote, proposing an economic combination that would turn the theory of property against property. Written to a prospective collaborator in conciliatory language, it remains the clearest first-hand statement of why they could not work together.

## The break

Proudhon published *System of Economical Contradictions, or The Philosophy of Poverty* in 1846 and 1847. Its method treated value as a contradictory social relation, with use value and exchange value moving in opposite directions, and proposed a law of proportionality of values in which justice meant that proportionality. This lay behind the later exchange-bank proposals. Marx answered in 1847 with *The Poverty of Philosophy*, whose foreword was a public verdict on a former collaborator: "M. Proudhon has the misfortune of being peculiarly misunderstood in Europe. In France, he has the right to be a bad economist, because he is reputed to be a good German philosopher." ([Karl Marx, foreword to *The Poverty of Philosophy*, Brussels, 15 June 1847, translation by the Institute of Marxism-Leninism, 1955](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1847/poverty-philosophy/foreword.htm)) Marx charged him with treating economic categories as timeless ideas and seeking an a priori solution to the social question. The tone was deliberately contemptuous, and the text was revised later by both men, so it is evidence of a judgment rather than a transparent report. It was not republished in full in Marx's lifetime.

## The bank and the right to work

February 1848 turned the critic into a journalist and deputy. Proudhon helped found *Le Représentant du Peuple*, which explained the scheme day by day. Its issue of 17 April described a bank that would exchange products without money: "By thus exchanging our products without the aid of cash, we would no longer be obliged to pay enormous interest to the usurers, the parasites who devour us." ([*Le Représentant du Peuple* no. 17, 17 April 1848, translation by Shawn P. Wilbur, 2026](https://www.libertarian-labyrinth.org/proudhon-library/le-representant-du-peuple-february-august-1848)) The pamphlet *The Solution of the Social Problem* gave the plan more fully: a Bank of Exchange issuing notes backed by products and bills rather than gold, interest only as an administrative commission, mutual guarantees, local credit committees and a draft constitution for a hundred thousand subscribers. These were published proposals with dates, addresses and recruitment notices. The paper declared economic reform everything and political reform nothing.

How far the Bank of the People, its practical form, got is a documentary problem. The Maitron dictionary reports 862 adherents and 1,897 shares in the first two days and nearly 20,000 subscribers with about 100,000 francs subscribed six weeks later. K. Steven Vincent gives a smaller figure, perhaps 13,267 subscribers and about 17,993 francs in receipts, and calls the enterprise stillborn. The figures measure different things at different dates: subscribers against shareholders, pledged against paid capital. They should not be merged. The collapse is agreed, its cause unsettled; imprisonment is the usual explanation, yet the small paid-up capital is visible in the figures. Engels, writing from Paris late in 1848, recorded that workers elected Proudhon to the Assembly and found him remarkable even when they could not follow him, though his judgments on his economics were those of an opponent.

His journalism puts him inside the argument over the right to work. Attacking Malthus's population doctrine, the press, the Church and the government, he wrote: "The theory of Malthus is the theory of political murder; of murder from motives of philanthropy and for love of God." ([Proudhon, "The Malthusians, the Representatives of the People", 1848](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/economics/proudhon/1848/malthusians.html)) He accused the state of refusing work to a hundred thousand people and then asking for a law to transport the insurgents after the civil war. The causal claims are his interpretation, made by a man facing prosecution for his writing.

## The limits of his socialism

During the crisis Proudhon urged workers to organise their own relief. Writing to Jeanne Deroin, a seamstress, feminist organiser and editor of *L'Opinion des femmes*, he asked: "Let women sustain one another, let them create relief funds; in addition, let them continue to labor, even at a reduced price, for it will be better to gain five cents than to do nothing" ([Proudhon to Jeanne Deroin, 1848](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/economics/proudhon/1848/letter-to-jeanne-deroin.html)) The tone is practical and paternalist; the same letter describes his larger aim as restoring the family, and Deroin's reply does not appear with it. His socialism reached a clear limit here. The Maitron dictionary is candid about his hostility to women's political equality: his appeal for women's self-organisation did not extend to a demand for their rights as citizens.

He opposed the June insurrection and later treated the republic as a broken pledge. In prison he wrote: "All men are born free and equal;—society is therefore by nature self-governing, i.e. ungovernable; and he who lays his hand on me to govern me is a usurper and tyrant; my declared enemy." ([Proudhon, "The Confessions of a Revolutionist", *The Spirit of the Age*, 2 February 1850, translated or summarised from the London *Weekly Tribune*](https://www.libertarian-labyrinth.org/proudhon-library/p-j-proudhon-confessions-of-a-revolutionist-spirit-of-the-age-1850/)) The English version says his proposal for organised tax refusal was rejected and cost him a fine and three years' imprisonment. In *The General Idea of the Revolution in the Nineteenth Century*, written in 1851, he looked back on the sequence: "The people demanded work, their protest went no further. They embraced the republican cause with ardor, those who had just proclaimed the Republic in their names having promised to give them work." ([Proudhon, *The General Idea of the Revolution in the Nineteenth Century*, 1851, translation by John Beverley Robinson, 1923](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/pierre-joseph-proudhon-the-general-idea-of-the-revolution-in-the-19th-century)) He called this a speculative plan rather than a record of events.

Marx's last word was not simple dismissal. In a letter of 24 January 1865 he wrote: "His speech in the National Assembly, however little insight it showed into existing conditions, was worthy of every praise. After the June insurrection it was an act of great courage." ([Karl Marx, letter to J. B. Schweizer, 24 January 1865, MECW/Selected Works translation](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1865/letters/65_01_24.htm)) In the same letter he called the people's bank a petty-bourgeois fantasy and said he had not joined the cry of treachery. He admitted that he had no books of Proudhon's to hand. The split was about method and politics, but also personal; neither man's retrospective account is a neutral measure of the other. George Woodcock reads Proudhon as the first deliberate anarchist and treats the break with Marx as the origin of socialism's libertarian and authoritarian divide ([Woodcock, 1967](https://www.ditext.com/woodcock/proudhon.html)). K. Steven Vincent rejects the familiar charge of mere inconsistency and argues for a persistent social programme beneath the shifts in tactic ([Vincent, 1984](https://sites.ohio.edu/chastain/ip/proudhon.htm)).

## What the record cannot show

Almost every source was written by Proudhon, Marx, Engels or a translator working on their texts. Jeanne Deroin appears mostly through a letter addressed to her. No member of the Bank of the People, no delegate to the workers' associations and no Proudhonist participant in the June Days speaks here for himself. Translation changes the argument: Tucker's "robbery" renders the French *vol*, several archive items name no translator, Robinson's 1923 version turns *anarchie* into "chaos," and the 1850 *Confessions* is closer to a summary than a translation. No physical archive was visited; the full French newspaper runs, the fourteen-volume correspondence and the notebooks remain unconsulted. The sources also do not measure outcomes: whether exchange notes circulated, whether workers gained tools or credit, or how many people depended on the associations. Mutualism has to be described as a published proposal and a partial undertaking, neither a working alternative suppressed before it could be judged nor a scheme whose failure has a single cause.
