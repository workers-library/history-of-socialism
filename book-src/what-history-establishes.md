This book has kept four things apart: what thinkers wrote, what revolutionaries intended, what states declared, and what happened to the people who lived under them. A constitution can declare that an economy works in a certain way without establishing that it does ([Marx, 1859 preface](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1859/critique-pol-economy/preface.htm)).

## Ideas: what socialists believed and why

Historical materialism holds that social existence shapes consciousness and that productive forces eventually conflict with existing relations of production. Marx's *Critique of the Gotha Programme* set a lower phase, distribution according to work, and a higher phase, distribution according to needs; Lenin read the first as socialism. Mao made contradiction central, Juche asserted national self-reliance in revolution, and Cabral insisted that theory must grow from a country's own social reality. These are arguments by named writers, not evidence that the society described was achieved ([Gotha Programme](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1875/gotha/ch01.htm); [Soviet *Political Economy*](https://www.marxists.org/subject/economy/authors/pe/pe-ch26.htm); [Cabral, 1966](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/cabral/1966/weapon-theory.htm)).

## Power: who controlled decisions

The vanguard is the most politically organised section of a movement. Lenin held that workers develop only trade-union consciousness by themselves, while democratic centralism means that members discuss policy but carry out the central decision. Luxemburg replied that freedom for one party alone is no freedom; Kollontai called bureaucracy a negation of mass self-activity. The constitutional texts agree on formal authority: the 1977 Soviet constitution made the party the directing force, and China's Article 1, Vietnam's Article 4 and Cuba's Article 5 all give it the leading role. Yugoslav councils were elected, but the plan and appointed director remained. The decisions that ended the USSR were taken by a few ministers and republican leaders ([Lenin, 1902](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1901/witbd/ii.htm); [Luxemburg, 1918](https://www.marxists.org/archive/luxemburg/1918/russian-revolution/ch06.htm); [Kollontai](https://www.marxists.org/archive/kollonta/1921/workers-opposition/ch01.htm); [Basic Law, Yugoslavia, 1950](https://www.marxists.org/subject/yugoslavia/self-management/1950/06/x01.htm); [Belovezh Accords](https://eccis.org/en/page/176)).

## Institutions: how principles were implemented

Planning was taught as an "economic law of socialism". The instruments changed: requisitioning gave way to the tax in kind and markets under NEP; workers' councils replaced direct state administration in Yugoslavia; China's constitution admitted a socialist market economy; Vietnam named a socialist oriented market economy; Cuba's Reordering Task unified currencies while keeping public ownership of the main means of production. Stalin's 1936 remark that a programme speaks of the future while a constitution should speak of the present is a warning in reverse: constitutional claims have to be checked ([Stalin on the draft constitution](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1936/11/25.htm); [China's constitution](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/lawsregulations/201911/20/content_WS5ed8856ec6d0b3f0e9499913.html); [Vietnam's constitution](https://www.constituteproject.org/constitution/Socialist_Republic_of_Vietnam_2013)).

## Outcomes: what happened and to whom

Outcomes were distributed unequally. Cuba's literacy campaign sharply reduced illiteracy and its volunteers remembered it as a change in their own lives; the same years saw camps for homosexuals, religious believers and political nonconformists ([Felo Rojas](https://www.mtpr.org/arts-culture/2015-03-22/kids-teaching-adults-how-to-read-and-write-three-cuban-brigadistas); [Elvy](https://acontracorriente.chass.ncsu.edu/index.php/acontracorriente/article/view/183)). Collectivisation and requisitioning were followed by the Ukrainian famine; demographers put excess deaths at about 3.9 million, with estimates from 2.6 to 5 million, while concealment is documented and intent contested ([Rudnytskyi and others](https://journals.library.ualberta.ca/csp/index.php/csp/article/view/21772); [Davies and Wheatcroft](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1057/9780230273979)). China's Great Leap famine has been estimated at about 30 million premature deaths, with weather and policy weighed differently ([Ashton and others](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/famine-in-china-1958-61/); [Bernstein](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/china-quarterly/article/mao-zedong-and-the-famine-of-19591960-a-study-in-wilfulness/3CF47A28C732B4365D2413C88813DC6E)). North Korean death estimates range from hundreds of thousands to two and a half million ([Haggard and Noland](https://www.piie.com/bookstore/famine-north-korea-markets-aid-and-reform)). These are estimates from different methods and baselines, not a single count; the chapter on North Korea sets out the underlying figures. The Chinese and North Korean figures are not measured on the same basis as the Ukrainian demographic series. East German workers marched in 1953 over work quotas; Soviet industry damaged the Aral Sea, and Chernobyl's liquidators were not told their doses ([Morgun](https://time.com/archive/6701510/planet-of-the-year-the-greening-of-the-u-s-s-r/); [Kramarevskyy](http://www.1986.org.ua/en/archive/memories)). Chinese officials report 98.99 million rural residents lifted out of poverty by 2020, measured against China's own poverty line ([Chinese white paper](https://english.www.gov.cn/atts/stream/files/606bc6cac6d0cc300eea77be)).

## Transformation: why movements and institutions changed

Movements changed when their arrangements failed to deliver what they promised. The 1921 retreat followed civil war, crop failure and Kronstadt; de-Stalinization reopened the argument over what socialism meant; the Sino-Soviet split redrew orthodoxy; 1968 and the New Left shifted the argument; reform in China and Vietnam followed economic crisis; 1989 to 1991 ended the bloc through a failed coup, republican sovereignty and decisions by named actors ([CPC 1963 Proposal](https://www.marxists.org/history/international/comintern/sino-soviet-split/cpc/proposal.htm); [National Security Archive briefing book](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/russia-programs/2021-12-21/end-soviet-union-1991)).

## Established historical findings

The documents exist, were written by the named people at the dates given, and record incompatible programmes. The Hague votes and the 1960 Statement are documents, not reconstructions ([Hague resolutions](https://www.marxists.org/history/international/iwma/documents/1872/hague-conference/resolutions.htm)). The literacy campaign, the Ukrainian famine, the 1953 East German uprising, the return of markets after 1921 and 1986, and the dissolution of the USSR in December 1991 are established events. Disagreement about their meaning does not make any of them contested.

## Recurring institutional patterns

One-party rule was paired with public ownership of major assets; planning was revised rather than abandoned when it failed; the leading party was written into constitutions while assemblies were formally sovereign; opposition from within the left was described as deviation or revisionism. Where markets returned, they did so under party rule and with socialist language intact. These are patterns in these cases, not laws; the three market forms differ.

## Context-dependent outcomes

The same device produced different results. The household contract raised Vietnamese output but redistributed work within villages; NEP revived trade in Moscow while villagers were still starving; literacy campaigning in Cuba enlarged women's expectations while the state confined political dissent. China's poverty figures measure a defined threshold and cannot be compared directly with Cuban rationing or North Korean famine estimates. Where evidence is thin, as with Yugoslav factory councils, no outcome can be stated with confidence.

## Unresolved scholarly debates

Scholars disagree over the Sino-Soviet split, with Lüthi giving ideology primacy and others stressing security, national interest or personal rivalry; over whether the Ukrainian famine was intended as mass killing; over the weight of weather and central policy in China's famine; over whether Luxemburg's criticisms were farsighted or destructive; over the causes of the Soviet collapse; and over whether Cabral was a Marxist in any orthodox sense. These arguments rest on different evidence and are not equally supported in every case ([Lüthi](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/30313)).

## Failed predictions

The 1960 Statement declared that the restoration of capitalism in the socialist countries had been "made socially and economically impossible"; the USSR ceased to exist in 1991. The Soviet textbook taught that categories such as capital, surplus value and wage labour would cease to exist under socialism, yet markets, prices and wage incentives returned. Kim Il Sung said in 1982 that the DPRK economy was "unaffected by world economic fluctuations" before the mid-1990s famine. Guevara's "new man" was an ambition rather than a dated forecast. A failed prediction is evidence about a claim, not a verdict on a whole tradition ([1960 Statement](https://www.marxists.org/history/international/comintern/sino-soviet-split/other/1960statement.htm); [Kim Il Sung](https://www.marxists.org/archive/kim-il-sung/2020/20200729120144.pdf); [Guevara, 1965](https://www.marxists.org/archive/guevara/1965/03/man-socialism.htm)).

## Successful predictions

Few statements here are testable forecasts that later succeeded. Engels warned in 1876 that each victory over nature brings a revenge; the Aral and Baikal cases fit that warning, though fitting a case is weaker than predicting it. Luxemburg argued in 1918 that freedom confined to one party ceases to be freedom; later one-party rule supports the institutional warning without proving her causal account. Bukharin's 1924 claim that conflict between worker and peasant was possible was borne out by the scissors crisis. These are partial confirmations, not validated theories.

## Open questions

How often did Yugoslav workers' councils actually control production? What did Nepmen and peasants think of the tax in kind? How were the gains of Vietnam's household contracts divided by gender? What weight should be given to ideology against security in the Sino-Soviet split? China's 2035 target for a high-standard socialist market economy cannot yet be judged. These questions require sources the book has not opened.

## Levels of certainty

The book uses eight grades. **Established** means supported by the cited record. **Strongly supported** means several independent sources converge. **Probable** means the best available evidence favours a conclusion while leaving room for doubt. **Contested** means credible scholars reach different conclusions from evidence that does not settle the matter. **Interpretive** means a reading of established facts whose framework is itself argued, such as state capitalism against a degenerated workers' state. **Unverified** means a claim repeated in the record but not checked here. **Uncertain** means the evidence is too thin to grade, as with the North Korean death toll. **Refuted** means a specific claim contradicted by the cited evidence. Political disagreement does not make a well-supported fact contested. This book has not been externally reviewed; these grades describe its own evidence, not a certification of certainty.

## Limits of the book's method

This is a URL and text check, not archival research. Most quotations reach readers in English translation, often with unnamed translators. The record leans toward officials, party theorists, journalists and memoirists; ordinary people appear mainly when a leader, a policeman or an oral historian recorded them. Missing originals and image-only scans carry into this synthesis. The book does not claim to have settled which interpretation of socialism is correct, nor to have measured living standards across societies with different statistics ([Soviet collapse record](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/russia-programs/2021-12-21/end-soviet-union-1991)).
