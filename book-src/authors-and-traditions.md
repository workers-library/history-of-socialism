# Authors and Intellectual Traditions

The historical chapters of this book explain what happened. This collection asks what particular thinkers argued about why it happened. It brings together socialist intellectuals, political economists, historians, anti-imperialist writers and critics of capitalism, and it records where they agree, where they disagree and what can be said for their claims with evidence.

A profile here is not a verdict. A writer's political interpretation is reported as their interpretation. Where a writer is a professional historian or economist, the method and evidence behind the work are described as well as the conclusion. Polemical writers sit beside archival historians and empirical economists, and the difference is stated rather than hidden. This follows the book's [Editorial & Source Policy](/editorial-source-policy).

## How a profile is built

Every author profile uses the same thirteen sections: biography; intellectual tradition; major contributions; analysis of capitalism; analysis of socialism; complete bibliography; recommended reading; selected lectures and interviews; intellectual influences; influence and legacy; criticisms and controversies; related thinkers; and sources cited grouped by evidentiary tier. The recommended-reading section selects three to five starting points and is kept separate from the complete bibliography, so a reader can see both where to begin and what the author published. The full rule is in `research/AUTHOR-PROFILES-STANDARD.md` in the repository.

Each profile is written from a research dossier in `research/authors/`. The dossier records which primary sources were opened, which facts could not be verified and where a claim comes from a secondary writer rather than the author. Profiles are marked `research-stage` until that check is complete. Nothing in this collection uses Wikipedia, Goodreads, Scribd or Google Books as evidence.

## Traditions represented

The collection is organised by argument, not by loyalty to a single tradition. The headings below describe the ground the collection is intended to cover; not every heading has a published profile yet.

- **Classical Marxism** — Marx, Engels and the writers who developed or revised their theory.
- **Marxism-Leninism** — the tradition of Lenin, Stalin and Mao, and its later interpreters.
- **Western Marxism** — Lukács, Gramsci, Adorno, Horkheimer and the critical-theory current.
- **Democratic socialism and social democracy** — parliamentary and reformist traditions.
- **Libertarian socialism and anarchism** — Bakunin, Kropotkin, Goldman, Bookchin and their critics.
- **Anti-imperialist thought** — Fanon, Rodney, Cabral, Nkrumah, Amin and the dependency writers.
- **Socialist political economy** — value, accumulation, monopoly, finance and the labour process.
- **Socialist feminism** — domestic labour, social reproduction and racial capitalism.
- **Black radical thought** — Du Bois, Robeson, Davis, Cedric Robinson and the prison abolitionists.
- **Ecological socialism** — the metabolic rift, fossil capital and degrowth.
- **Contemporary critical thought** — media, ideology, platform capital and the claim that alternatives are unthinkable.

## Reading the disagreements

Chomsky approaches socialism from a libertarian direction; Parenti and Losurdo are more sympathetic to historical communist projects. Herman's propaganda model is a more formal account of the media mechanism Parenti describes through ownership. Rodney, Fanon and Amin write from the anti-colonial tradition that the book's capitalism and empire chapters draw on. The collection keeps these differences visible. A reader who wants to know which claims survive contact with the evidence should follow the sources listed at the end of each profile and the historical chapters linked from them.
