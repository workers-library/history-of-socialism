In 1919 Nguyễn Ái Quoc asked the Paris Peace Conference for civil rights inside the French empire, among them "Complete amnesty of Vietnamese political prisoners" ([claim](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/ho-chi-minh/works/1919/0001.htm)). In 1945 his movement took power in Hanoi in the gap after Japan's surrender ([Trường Chinh](https://archive.org/download/the-august-revolution-truong-chinh-1958/TheAugustRevolution-TruongChinh-1958_djvu.txt)). The puzzle is that Marx and Engels wrote for industrial capitalism, with the proletariat defined in Engels's 1888 footnote as "the class of modern wage labourers who, having no means of production of their own, are reduced to selling their labour power in order to live" ([*Manifesto*, ch. I](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1848/communist-manifesto/ch01.htm)), while the victorious movements worked chiefly where that class was small.

Marx's 1859 preface warned that "no social order is ever destroyed before all the productive forces for which it is sufficient have been developed"; productive forces means the labour, tools, techniques and knowledge available ([preface](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1859/critique-pol-economy/preface.htm)). The *Manifesto* said its ten measures would apply "in most advanced countries" ([*Manifesto*, ch. II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1848/communist-manifesto/ch02.htm)). Lenin's answer was uneven development: monopoly capitalism, the division of the world among the great powers, and revolution possible in one country ([*Imperialism*, ch. VII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1916/imp-hsc/ch07.htm)). Stalin wrote in 1924 that Russia "was the focus of all these contradictions of imperialism" ([*Foundations of Leninism*, ch. I](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1924/foundations-leninism/ch01.htm)). These are arguments within a tradition, not findings about why particular revolts won.

The weight of the countryside is the first common feature. Ho told the Comintern in 1924 that "Ninety-five per cent of the population are peasants" in the French colonies ([Ho](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/ho-chi-minh/works/1924/07/08.htm)). In Hunan in 1927 Mao reported poor peasants at 70 per cent, middle peasants at 20 and landlords and rich peasants at 10, and claimed 1,367,727 members in peasant associations across thirty-seven counties ([Mao](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-1/mswv1_2.htm)). His class percentages came from surveys whose method he did not give, but any movement seeking rural support had to address rent and land. In Crow Village in 1946 farmer Wang told John Hersey that a "Clearing the Accounts" meeting returned thirty piculs of grain and cut his rent from twelve piculs a mow to eight; the landlord Hsi described mortgaging 130 of his 300 mou ([Hersey](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1946/07/27/the-communization-of-crow-village)). The 1947 Draft Agrarian Law declared the "feudal and semi-feudal" system abolished and "land-to-the-tiller" established. Hinton, sympathetic to the revolution, recorded a dozen people beaten to death and small holders wrongly dispossessed, and warned that Long Bow was atypical ([Hinton](https://ebin.pub/fanshen-a-documentary-of-revolution-in-a-chinese-village-9780520210400.html)). Land hunger was a force to be mobilised, not a uniform grievance that movements simply expressed.

Castro's 1953 defence counted "the five hundred thousand farm laborers who live in miserable shacks, who work four months of the year and starve the rest" and 100,000 small farmers working land that was not theirs ([*History Will Absolve Me*](https://www.marxists.org/history/cuba/archive/castro/1953/10/16.htm)). The 1959 agrarian law broke the large estates while forbidding "spontaneous seizures" ([Castro](https://www.marxists.org/history/cuba/archive/castro/1959/05/17.htm)). In Vietnam, Trường Chinh wrote that "Hundreds of thousands of people starved beside granaries full of rice kept by the Japanese and French" ([Trường Chinh](https://archive.org/download/the-august-revolution-truong-chinh-1958/TheAugustRevolution-TruongChinh-1958_djvu.txt)). The 1953 to 1956 land reform remains a gap: Giáp called it a source of army strength, while a RAND study based on prisoner interviews gave four incompatible death estimates and called its evidence "meager" ([Giáp](https://archive.org/download/dien-bien-phu-vo-nguyen-giap/Dien%20Bien%20Phu_djvu.txt); [RAND](https://archive.org/details/arnsten-leites-land-reform-in-rural-vietnam)).

War broke old states. Russia's February Revolution produced dual power, two authorities neither strong enough to displace the other, and Lenin's April Theses refused support to the Provisional Government ([dual power](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1917/apr/09.htm); [April Theses](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1917/apr/04.htm)). China's Long March left the Red Army weakened; Mao claimed "more than twenty thousand *li*" through eleven provinces ([Mao](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-1/mswv1_11.htm)). The Japanese invasion and Chiang's detention at Xi'an in December 1936 pushed the struggle towards a united front. The American ambassador cabled that he lacked information from Xi'an as to the generals' purposes, while Mao claimed Communist mediation was decisive ([Johnson](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1936v04/d418); [Mao](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-1/mswv1_13.htm)). Cuba's courts had already failed Batista's opponent: Castro filed a writ in 1952 and found that "Days and months passed... The accused remained unmolested" ([*History Will Absolve Me*](https://www.marxists.org/history/cuba/archive/castro/1953/10/16.htm)). Vietnam's unanswered petition and the Nationalist inflation and corruption that Suzanne Pepper studied point the same way ([Pepper](https://www.inlibra.com/en/document/view/detail/uuid/93a56d7c-36a4-3e9a-9345-575dc8c001f8)). Weak institutions did not cause the revolutions, but they offered little to defend and few legal routes for change.

Organisation mattered as much as grievance. Lenin argued that workers "exclusively by its own effort, is able to develop only trade-union consciousness" and called for professional revolutionaries ([*What Is To Be Done?*](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1901/witbd/ii.htm)); in 1920 he described the party guiding the soviets, with 611,000 members and over four million in non-party unions ([Lenin](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1920/lwc/ch10.htm)). Ho founded the Indochinese Communist Party in 1930 as "the Party of the working class" ([founding appeal](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/ho-chi-minh/works/1930/02/18.htm)). At Zunyi in January 1935 the Party chronicle records the Red Army falling from over 86,000 to just over 30,000 and Mao entering the Standing Committee, though historians dispute how decisive the meeting was ([*Long March*](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-1/mswv1_11.htm)). Castro said in 1961 that "you cannot make a revolution, and above all, you cannot carry a revolution forward without a strong and disciplined organization", and that power was never expected with "ten, twelve or a hundred men" ([Castro](https://www.marxists.org/history/cuba/archive/castro/1961/12/02.htm)); the movements merged into the ORI, then the PURS, then the Communist Party of Cuba ([Castro](https://www.marxists.org/history/cuba/archive/castro/1965/10/03-oct-1965.htm)). Guevara's foco thesis held that "the insurrection itself can create" favourable conditions and that the countryside should be the main battleground; Bolivia ended with his death ([Guevara](https://www.marxists.org/archive/guevara/1963/09/guerrilla-warfare.htm); [*Global Survey*](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/cabral/index.htm)).

The book's own sources disagree about weight. Johnson argued from captured Japanese records that invasion produced the peasant nationalism the Chinese Communists harnessed; Selden answered from base-area records that rent reduction, cooperatives and the three-thirds system had won support earlier ([Johnson](https://www.sup.org/books/asian-studies/peasant-nationalism-and-communist-power); [Selden](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/mono/10.4324/9781315286419/china-revolution-mark-selden)). Pepper shifted attention to Nationalist failures in the cities ([Pepper](https://www.inlibra.com/en/document/view/detail/uuid/93a56d7c-36a4-3e9a-9345-575dc8c001f8)). The *Global Survey of the Socialist Camp* records a parallel argument: Lüthi gives ideology the primary role in the Sino-Soviet split, while others stress security and national interest ([Lüthi](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/30313); [Wilson Center](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/event/the-sino-soviet-split-1956-1966-the-cold-war-the-communist-world)). Lüthi wrote about the split, so he cannot settle this question; the point is that single-cause accounts are contestable in this literature. Ferrer argues that Cuba became socialist after taking power, against the leadership's own account, and de la Cova treats Moncada as a defeat recast as victory ([Ferrer](https://www.adaferrer.net/cuba-an-american-history); [de la Cova](https://www.latinamericanstudies.org/academic/Moncada-order.pdf)).

## Limits of the record

What is established is documented rural inequality, state breakdown under war, disciplined organisations, and peasant participation that sometimes brought rent cuts and land transfers, alongside coercion and wrongful dispossession. There is no comparative dataset, no pre-victory survey of village opinion and no independent measure of support. Most voices are leaders, foreign correspondents and retrospective participants in translation; the record has no peasant testimony from the Vietnamese land reform, no village debate about a Chinese commune and no rank-and-file Cuban mill-worker oral history ([RAND](https://archive.org/details/arnsten-leites-land-reform-in-rural-vietnam); [Hinton](https://ebin.pub/fanshen-a-documentary-of-revolution-in-a-chinese-village-9780520210400.html); [Elvy](https://acontracorriente.chass.ncsu.edu/index.php/acontracorriente/article/view/183)). No counterfactual can be tested. The sources support converging circumstances in particular places, not a law of revolution.

For the documentary basis see [*The Theoretical Backbone*](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1859/critique-pol-economy/preface.htm), [*Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels*](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1848/communist-manifesto/ch01.htm), [*Vladimir Lenin, Joseph Stalin and the Soviet Experience*](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1916/imp-hsc/ch07.htm), [*The Long March and the Chinese Civil War*](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-1/mswv1_11.htm), [*Land to the Tiller*](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-1/mswv1_2.htm), [*The Vietnamese revolution, 1919–1954*](https://archive.org/download/the-august-revolution-truong-chinh-1958/TheAugustRevolution-TruongChinh-1958_djvu.txt), [*After the Victory*](https://www.adaferrer.net/cuba-an-american-history), [*Fidel Castro*](https://www.marxists.org/history/cuba/archive/castro/1961/12/02.htm) and [*Global Survey of the Socialist Camp*](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/30313).
