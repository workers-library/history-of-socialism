# Political compass: a proposed ten-axis expansion

**Status.** This document is a design proposal only. It does not report a validated instrument, and nothing here has been piloted. It accompanies, and does not replace, [the five-axis research note](political-compass-research.md) and [the validation notes](political-compass-validation-notes.md). No existing file was changed.

The brief is to move from 5 axes / 30 items to 10 axes / 100 items: five new axes that are conceptually distinct from Economy, Authority, Community, Change and Scope; ten balanced items per axis; and a display that survives twenty poles. The guiding constraint remains the one stated in the original research: the useful question is which distinctions the map preserves and which it erases. Adding axes is not progress if the new axes simply re-measure old ones with different words.

## 1. Selecting the five additional axes

I evaluated eleven candidates. The table states the decision and the main overlap risk. "Accept" means the axis earns a separate coordinate under a *narrow operational definition*; several accepted axes would fail if defined loosely.

| Candidate | Decision | Main overlap risk and resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Religious ↔ Secular | **Accept** | Change already lists religion among inherited norms. Resolve by narrowing Religion to the *institutional role of religious authority in public law and education*, and moving the v1 item "Religious leaders should be given seats in the legislature" out of Change into Religion. Personal piety and traditional observance stay on Change. |
| Egalitarian ↔ Elitist (class/elite structure) | Reject | Economy already covers ownership, distribution and acceptance of class hierarchy. "Elitist" also conflates wealth with expertise, which belongs to Governance. Adding it would double-count Economy. |
| Growth-oriented ↔ Ecological | **Accept** | No existing axis concerns the human–nature relation or limits to production. Distinct from Economy, which asks who controls output, not how much output there should be. |
| Hierarchical ↔ Egalitarian gender roles | Reject | Change explicitly includes gender roles and sexual conduct. A gender axis would split one construct. If added, gender examples would first have to be removed from Change; that is a redesign, not an addition. |
| Participatory ↔ Technocratic | **Accept** | Distinct from Authority (coercion and civil liberties) and Economy (ownership). It measures *who is entitled to make binding decisions* and on what basis. Items must name decision procedures, not repression or property. |
| Populist ↔ Pluralist | **Accept** | Risk of collapsing into Authority, since populism is often authoritarian in practice. Resolve by defining it as a *style of political representation*: direct expression of a unified people versus bargaining among legitimate competing groups. Items avoid speech, policing and detention. |
| Retributive ↔ Restorative | **Accept** | Authority's definition currently mentions punishment. Resolve by scoping Justice to *sentencing philosophy and response to wrongdoing* (courts, victims, offenders, repair), with no items on state coercion, prisons as arbitrary detention, or civil liberties. |
| Technocratic ↔ Anti-expert (science/evidence) | Fold into Governance | A separate "anti-expert" axis would partly duplicate Governance and invite a straw-man item set. The pro-expert pole is already sampled by Governance items; the opposite pole is better written as demands for lay decision rights, which are the participatory items. |
| Centralised ↔ Localist (regionalism) | Reject | Overlaps Authority (concentrated state power) and Governance (who decides). Territorial scale is better treated as item content inside those axes than as a tenth dimension. |
| Market ↔ Planning | Reject | Already the core of Economy. The user's brief correctly rules it out. |
| Migration ↔ Citizenship | Reject as a separate axis | Already sampled by Scope (asylum, immigration, loyalty to humanity). Adding it would fragment Scope without new conceptual content. |

### The five accepted additions, with their operational content

**5. Ecology — Growth-oriented ↔ Ecological.** The negative pole privileges increases in output, extraction and employment; the positive pole privileges ecological limits, pollution control and restoration. The distinction is not "left versus right": socialists have historically divided between productivist and ecological currents, and Foster's reconstruction of Marx's environmental writings exists precisely because the productivist reading was not the only one. Survey work on the New Environmental Paradigm supplies an established measurement tradition for environmental orientations (Dunlap and Van Liere 1978; Stern, Dietz and Kalof 1993). *Why it earns a place:* a compass that cannot separate a growth-oriented trade unionist from a green socialist erases a real division within socialism itself. *Overlap risk:* item 8 of Ecology ("Public transport fares should be subsidised to cut car use") could look like an Economy redistribution item; it is keyed to Ecological because the stated mechanism is emissions, not transfers. Pilot data must test that loading.

**6. Governance — Participatory ↔ Technocratic.** The negative pole gives binding decision rights to residents, workers or their assemblies; the positive pole gives them to trained experts and independent commissions. Pateman (1970) argues that elitist theories of democracy rest on an inadequate reading of democratic theory; Dahl (1989) supplies the broader case for contestation and participation; Fischer (1990) analyses expertise as a political claim rather than a neutral input. *Why it earns a place:* the split between council democracy, syndicalism and expert planning is one of the oldest arguments in the socialist tradition, and the two-axis chart hides it by filing everything under "authority". *Overlap risk:* "Workers should elect the managers of their workplace" is about decision rights, not ownership, but respondents may read it as an Economy item. Keep Governance items off ownership and wages; keep Economy items off who chairs the board. The v1 Economy item about worker councils appointing company boards was replaced for this reason.

**7. Religion — Religious ↔ Secular.** The negative pole gives religious authorities a formal role in state institutions; the positive pole reserves public offices, schools and courts for civil authority. Bruce's defence of secularization theory (2011) and the *Oxford Handbook of Secularism* (Zuckerman and Shook 2017) provide the conceptual literature; the Traditional/Secular-rational dimension in Inglehart and Welzel (2005) provides the survey background, with the caveat recorded in the main research note that its country scores are not a template for individuals. *Why it earns a place:* Christian socialism, Catholic social teaching, Jewish and Muslim socialist currents, and militant anti-clerical Marxism cannot be told apart on Change alone if "religious" is treated as one end of a single traditionalism scale. *Overlap risk:* high. The mitigation is a strict institutional definition. Items about personal belief, observance or inherited custom belong to Change; items about legislatures, schools, family courts and the head of state belong here.

**8. Representation — Populist ↔ Pluralist.** The negative pole treats elected leaders as the direct voice of a unified people and treats rival parties as dispensable; the positive pole treats parties, unions and courts as legitimate competing institutions with procedural checks. Mudde (2004) defines populism as a thin ideology that splits society into a pure people and a corrupt elite; Mudde and Rovira Kaltwasser (2017) give the comparative treatment. *Why it earns a place:* left-wing populism and pluralist social democracy are both present in the history of socialism, and neither Authority nor Economy records the difference. *Overlap risk:* populist movements are frequently authoritarian, so this axis will correlate with Authority. That is a hypothesis to test, not a reason to delete the axis; if the correlation approaches .8 in a pilot, merge them. Items deliberately avoid nation, borders and outsiders so the axis does not become a second Scope.

**9. Justice — Retributive ↔ Restorative.** The negative pole treats sentencing as proportionate payment for harm; the positive pole treats it as repair, mediation and reintegration. Rusche and Kirchheimer's *Punishment and Social Structure* (1939) is a foundational Marxist study of penal policy, which makes the axis directly relevant to this site; Zehr (1990) supplies the restorative-justice alternative. *Why it earns a place:* penal reform is a persistent socialist concern and is invisible on a chart that only asks whether the state may detain people. *Overlap risk:* moderate. Authority currently lists "punishment" in its definition. The operational fix is to remove punitive-coercion items from Authority and keep Justice items inside courts and sentencing. If a respondent reads "Prison should be the main response to serious theft" as an Authority item, the wording has failed and must be rewritten after piloting.

## 2. Item-writing rules applied

Each axis has ten statements, five keyed **−1** (toward the first pole) and five keyed **+1** (toward the second pole), so that agreement bias cannot move a whole axis (Pasek and Krosnick 2010, as summarised in the validation notes). Every statement is single-barrelled, names a concrete institution or action, and keeps the actor stable within an axis. I removed the flagged constructions: no "whatever", "as much as possible" or "even when"; no vague "common good"; no double negatives. A few retained v1 items contain a single "no" or "without" where the negation is the claim itself (for example, "There should be no legal limit on how much wealth a person may own"); these were already accepted in the validation notes and are marked below as retained. Three v1 items were replaced: the Change item on religious legislators moved to Religion; the Change item on religious leaders in the legislature was replaced with hereditary head of state; the Scope aid item lost its "even if" framing; and the Economy worker-board item was replaced with state ownership of railways to protect the Governance distinction.

## 4. The visualization problem

The current result view draws a ten-spoke radar: five axes, each with two poles at opposite ends. Ten axes would put twenty pole labels around the circle at 18-degree intervals. At the existing 460-unit viewBox with roughly 150 units of radius, the arc between labels is about 47 units — shorter than most pole names. Labels overlap, the polygon becomes a star whose area changes with axis ordering, and the reader cannot tell which spoke is which without cross-referencing the score list. A radar chart also implies that area or shape is meaningful; with bipolar axes it is not.

**Recommendation: a diverging bar list as the primary display.** Ten rows, one per axis, each a horizontal bar centred on zero with the negative pole named at the left end and the positive pole at the right end, the numeric score printed once, and an uncertainty band drawn over the bar. Rows can be grouped (material: Economy, Ecology, Governance; institutional: Authority, Representation, Justice; cultural: Community, Change, Religion, Scope) or sorted by absolute score, but grouping should be fixed and disclosed rather than chosen by the data. This display keeps every pole legible on a phone, degrades gracefully to plain text for the "copy result" function and screen readers, and makes gap comparisons against an illustrative profile a matter of reading ten numbers rather than judging two polygons.

Two defensible alternatives can supplement but not replace it. A **radial bar chart** uses the same twenty directions but draws bars from the centre rather than a polygon, with abbreviated pole labels (for example "Coll." / "Market") and the full label in the score list; it is compact but still suffers label crowding. **Small multiples** show two five-axis radar charts side by side (axes 0–4 and 5–9) with the same three-decade grid; this preserves the familiar shape while keeping ten labels per chart, at the cost of doubling the visual load and requiring an explicit warning that the two shapes are not comparable in area.

Whichever display is chosen, keep the L1 gap ranking from the validation notes rather than a "percentage similar": with ten dimensions the box-diagonal rescaling is even less defensible. If a total gap is shown, the range becomes 0–2000, and it should be labelled as a display convention against the distribution of the illustrative profiles, not a distance with empirical meaning.

## 5. Editorial caveats and next steps

- **No validation claim.** These ten axes are a hypothesis about which distinctions matter. The five new ones are even more provisional than the original five: their item loadings have not been estimated and their correlations with the existing axes are unknown. Keep "self-report heuristic, not a validated test" prominent on the result page.
- **Provisional profiles.** The seven `PROFILES` vectors have five coordinates and no provenance. They cannot simply be extended to ten by assertion. Each extension needs the text interpreted, a date, an assigner and a source, and must be versioned as an editorial hypothesis. Where a tradition contains opposing wings (social anarchism, socialism, conservatism), list both or state which wing is meant.
- **No false precision.** With ten items per axis, scoring becomes `sum / 20 * 100`, not `/ 12`. One step on the five-point response scale moves an axis by 5 points (a full −2 to +2 swing on one statement moves it by 20), not by 8.3 as in the 30-item version. Keep unrounded values internally; show integers only as a display convenience. Band labels ("Strongly", "Leans", "Mixed") remain conventions with no empirical cut-offs.
- **Burden and fatigue.** One hundred items is roughly 12–15 minutes, more than three times the current quiz. Consider offering a short form that samples four items per axis, but never score an axis from fewer than four without stating the rule and the wider uncertainty.
- **Pilot before publishing scores.** The validation plan's ~300 participants was sized for 30 items. A 100-item instrument needs on the order of 500–1,000 respondents for a credible EFA/CFA split. Report alpha and omega with intervals, test–retest ICC, inter-axis correlations, an acquiescence factor, and configural/loading/intercept invariance before any cross-language comparison. If Representation and Authority, or Governance and Economy, correlate around .8, the ten-axis picture overstates what was measured; merge or rewrite. Re-pilot after any wording change.
